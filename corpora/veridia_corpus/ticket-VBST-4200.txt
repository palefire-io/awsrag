[2025-09-16 08:05] Danielle Frost (Pathology Systems Analyst):
E-503 HL7 ACK not received on three VeriScan 400s at the MRI site, starting
overnight. Roughly 15% of messages. The other eleven analysers across the

Gateway logs show it's rejecting the messages with "duplicate message control

Nothing changed on the gateway. We did move three analysers between subnets

[2025-09-16 09:40] Aisha Rahman (Support Engineering L2):
Duplicate MSH-10 across a multi-instrument site is a known pattern and the
network move is almost certainly relevant, though not in the way you might
expect.

The instrument generates MSH-10 as SERIAL-YYYYMMDD-NNNN, where SERIAL is the
instrument serial number. That guarantees uniqueness across instruments
because the serial differs. If something in the path strips or rewrites the
serial prefix, you get collisions as soon as two instruments produce the same
sequence number on the same day - which they will, constantly.

Question: does your gateway apply any transformation to MSH-10, and did the
three moved analysers get pointed at a different gateway interface or a
different inbound channel?

[2025-09-16 11:20] Danielle Frost (Pathology Systems Analyst):
Checking... yes. The three moved analysers are now hitting inbound channel
HAEM-B instead of HAEM-A. HAEM-B has a message control ID rewrite rule that
HAEM-A doesn't - it truncates MSH-10 to the last 12 characters.

SERIAL-YYYYMMDD-NNNN truncated to 12 characters is YYYYMMDD-NNNN. Which drops
the serial.

That's it, isn't it.

[2025-09-16 11:45] Aisha Rahman (Support Engineering L2):
That's it. Three instruments, same date, sequence numbers overlapping, and the
one thing that made them unique has been truncated away.

The rule on HAEM-B was presumably added for a different instrument type with a
longer control ID. It's not wrong in itself; it's just wrong for ours.

Options, in order of preference:
1. Remove the truncation rule from HAEM-B, or exempt the VeriScan feeds from
   it.
2. Point the three analysers back at HAEM-A.
3. If you must truncate, take the FIRST 12 characters rather than the last -
   that keeps the serial and drops sequence digits, which is the wrong
   trade-off but at least fails differently.

Not an option: changing the MSH-10 format on the instrument. It isn't
configurable, and for good reason.

[2025-09-16 14:30] Danielle Frost (Pathology Systems Analyst):
Exempted the VeriScan feeds from the truncation rule on HAEM-B. E-503 stopped
within a minute.

We'd been chasing this as a network problem since Friday because the network
change was the obvious suspect. It was the network change, just not the part
we were looking at.

[2025-09-16 15:00] Aisha Rahman (Support Engineering L2):
Very common pattern - the change is the cause but the mechanism is two steps
away from the change.

For your documentation: the MSH-10 format and the uniqueness requirement are
in lookup-hl7-message-mapping.csv and in the integration spec
(spec-vericonnect-integration-v6.txt section 4, "Common causes of E-503"). It's
the first item on that list, which tells you how often this happens.

Two other things worth checking while you're in there, since they're the other
two common E-503 causes:
- whether HAEM-B validates OBR-7 against its own clock (relevant if you ever
  run an instrument on old firmware)
- whether the character set is set consistently across both channels

[2025-09-17 09:00] Danielle Frost (Pathology Systems Analyst):
Both checked, both fine. Closing. Thanks - fast and precise.

--- Resolution ---
Root cause: middleware inbound channel HAEM-B truncating MSH-10 to the last 12
characters, removing the instrument serial prefix that guarantees uniqueness.
Three analysers moved to that channel during a network refresh.
Actions: VeriScan feeds exempted from the truncation rule.
See also: spec-vericonnect-integration-v6.txt s.4,
lookup-hl7-message-mapping.csv
