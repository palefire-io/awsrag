[2025-06-05 16:30] Marcus Chen (QC Applications Specialist):
Looked at this carefully. Observations:

- The drift is real and consistent: about +1.8 fL over 14 days, then reset by
  calibration.
- It does not correlate with reagent lot changes - it continues across two D5
  lots and three L3 lots.
- It does not correlate with ambient.
- The applied calibration factor is stepping down by roughly the same amount
  each time, which means the instrument is drifting, not the control material.
- Your control lot is the same across the period and your other instruments
  using the same lot are stable, so it isn't the material.

That rules out the easy answers. What's left is something in the RBC aperture
or the pulse height analysis on this specific instrument.

Two things to try:
1. Extended Clean followed by an aperture burn cycle (Service > Maintenance >
   Aperture Burn), then monitor for 14 days without recalibrating.
2. Run patient specimen comparison against one of your stable instruments at
   day 1 and day 12 of a calibration cycle, 20 specimens each time. If the
   comparison shifts, the instrument is genuinely drifting. If it doesn't, the
   control material is behaving differently on this instrument than the others
   for some reason we haven't identified.
