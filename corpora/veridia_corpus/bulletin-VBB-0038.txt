VERIDIA BIOSYSTEMS - CUSTOMER ADVISORY BULLETIN
=====================================================
Reference: VBB-0038
Date Issued: 2025-01-24
Type: Technical Advisory (non-safety)
Affected Product: VeriPrep SP-1 Automated Sample Preparation Station
Affected Hardware Revision: SP-1r1 (all serial numbers SP1-R1-xxxx)
Not affected: SP-1r2 (from Q3 2025)

SUBJECT: Tube feed jams and barcode read failures with short-cap 5 mL tubes

-----------------------------------------------------
BACKGROUND
-----------------------------------------------------
Veridia has received 23 reports since September 2024 of VeriPrep SP-1 units
raising P-105 (tube height out of range), P-101 (barcode no decode) and, in the
more serious presentations, P-112 (tube feed mechanism jam) when processing
5 mL evacuated blood collection tubes fitted with a short-profile cap.

Investigation has established that the affected tubes sit 2.1 to 2.8 mm lower in
the carrier than the tube geometry the SP-1r1 feed mechanism and barcode window
were designed around. Two consequences follow:

  1. The barcode label may sit partly below the lower edge of the scanner
     window, producing a partial or failed decode (P-101 / P-103).

  2. The reduced cap height gives the feed pawl insufficient purchase. Under
     the retry behaviour introduced in SP-1 firmware 2.2.0, a tube that fails
     to index can tilt in the carrier and wedge against the guide rail. This
     is the P-112 jam.

The issue is a tolerance interaction, not a defect in either the instrument or
the tubes. Tubes from at least four suppliers fall within the affected
geometry. Veridia is not naming suppliers in this bulletin because the same
supplier may ship compliant and non-compliant cap profiles under one catalogue
number depending on manufacturing site.

-----------------------------------------------------
HOW TO IDENTIFY AFFECTED TUBES
-----------------------------------------------------
Measure the overall height of the capped tube. If the total height is less than
75.5 mm for a nominal 13 x 75 mm 5 mL tube, the tube is within the affected
geometry.

A simple check: place the capped tube in the SP-1 carrier and view from the
front. If the top of the barcode label is more than 1 mm below the top edge of
the scanner window, the tube is affected.

-----------------------------------------------------
MITIGATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------
Immediate (no cost):
  - Where possible, switch to a standard-cap 5 mL tube.
  - Apply barcode labels 3 mm higher than the current label position. Most
    laboratory printers allow a vertical offset in the label template.
  - Reduce carrier fill to alternate positions on affected racks. This does not
    prevent jams but reduces the chance of a cascade jam.

Interim (chargeable, low cost):
  - Order SP1-ADP-05, a set of 10 short-cap tube adapters (list GBP 72.00,
    lead time 3 days). The adapter raises the tube by 2.5 mm and restores both
    scanner alignment and feed pawl purchase. Adapters are washable and
    autoclavable at 121 C.

Permanent:
  - SP-1r2 units, shipping from Q3 2025, include a redesigned feed mechanism
    (SP1-MEC-141) with a wider tube height tolerance band and a repositioned
    scanner window. Internal validation over 5,000 tube presentations recorded
    a jam rate of 0.02 per cent against 1.8 per cent for r1 with affected
    tubes.
  - A retrofit kit (SP1-KIT-R2UP, list GBP 1,150.00) is available for r1 units.
    The retrofit requires an engineer visit of approximately 6 hours and
    includes the firmware 2.3.0 licence. Contact your account manager.

-----------------------------------------------------
CLEARING A P-112 JAM
-----------------------------------------------------
Refer to SOP-VBS-003 section 6. In summary: stop the run, open the lid (this
raises P-501, which is expected), release the carrier lock, withdraw the tube
by hand without rotating it, inspect the feed pawl and guide rail for shed cap
material, then run Gripper Home from the Service menu before resuming.

Do NOT attempt to free a wedged tube with the instrument running. Do not use
metal tools in the tube path.
