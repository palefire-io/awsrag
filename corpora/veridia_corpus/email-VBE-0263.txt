Tom,

VeriConnect 6.2 has been in for three weeks and my informatics colleagues have
been playing with the FHIR R4 export. They like it. They would like to use it.

The problem is "NOT FOR DIAGNOSTIC USE".

I understand why that statement is there. What I would like to understand is
what has to happen for it to go away, and roughly when.

The reason this matters to us specifically: our trust is part of a regional
shared care record programme that consumes FHIR. Today we transform our HL7 v2
into FHIR in a middleware layer that we maintain ourselves, badly, with one
developer who is leaving in September. A validated FHIR export from the source
system would let us delete that layer.

Concrete questions:

1. What is the validation gap? Is it a matter of running the conformance
   testing and updating the technical documentation, or is there a substantive
   question about whether the FHIR representation is equivalent to the HL7 v2
   one?

2. Does the Notified Body have to see it? My assumption is that changing the
   output format of a Class C device's data path is a change that needs
   assessing, but I do not know whether it falls inside the scope of the
   assessment you already have in flight.

3. Is there any intermediate position? For example, could we use the FHIR
   export for the shared care record, which is a read-only clinical summary,
   while continuing to use HL7 v2 for the primary result path into our LIS?
   That is arguably not a diagnostic use of the FHIR data at all.

4. If the answer to 3 is "ask your own regulatory people", say so and I will.
   But I would want to know Veridia's view first because our regulatory people
   will ask what the manufacturer says.

5. Timeline. Even a wide range would help. "This year" versus "not before
   2028" changes what we do about our departing developer.

I am not pushing you to say something you cannot support. I would genuinely
rather have "we do not know" than a date that slips.

Best,
