-----------------------------------------------------------------
1. SUMMARY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
VeriScan firmware 4.2.1, released 2025-02-03, emits HL7 timestamps that drift
forward relative to wall-clock time. The drift accumulates with instrument
uptime and resets on power cycle. Measured drift on the reference VeriScan 400
Pro reached 47 minutes after 14 days of continuous uptime. VeriScan 400
instruments running the same firmware drift by under 90 seconds over the same
period.

Root cause: the 4.2.1 refactor of the HL7 timestamp assembly replaced a direct
read of the real-time clock with a monotonic accumulator seeded at boot and
advanced by the result-cycle scheduler tick. The scheduler tick is nominally
100 ms but is extended by optical integration on the Pro's reticulocyte and
extended-flagging code paths. The accumulator therefore advanced faster than
real time, and the excess accumulated without bound.

Result values are not affected. Four timestamp fields are affected: OBR-7,
OBX-14, OBR-22 and MSH-7.

Firmware 4.2.2 was an incomplete correction and was withdrawn. Firmware 4.2.3
(2025-03-19) corrects the defect and adds drift telemetry.

3. TECHNICAL DETAIL
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Before 4.2.1, the routine hl7_assemble_timestamp() called rtc_read()
     directly for each of the four timestamp fields at the point of message
     assembly. This was correct but was measured at 1.8 ms per call because
     rtc_read() takes the I2C bus lock, and four calls per message on a
     high-throughput instrument was identified during 4.2.0 profiling as a
     contributor to the message assembly latency.

3.2  In 4.2.1 the call was replaced by a read of a value maintained in the
     result-cycle scheduler:

       t_now = boot_epoch + (tick_count * TICK_MS)

     where TICK_MS is the nominal scheduler period of 100 ms.

3.3  The assumption that tick_count * TICK_MS approximates elapsed real time
     holds only if every tick takes exactly TICK_MS. It does not. The
     scheduler is cooperative; a tick completes when the work scheduled in it
     completes. On the VeriScan 400 the work per tick is bounded and the mean
     tick duration measured 100.4 ms, giving a drift of about 0.4 per cent -
     approximately 84 seconds over 14 days.

3.4  On the VeriScan 400 Pro the reticulocyte integration window and, on
     4.3.0-precursor builds, the extended flagging computation extend the tick
     substantially. Measured mean tick duration on the Pro under a mixed
     workload was 123.3 ms, a 23.3 per cent excess. Over 14 days of continuous
     uptime this is 47.0 minutes, which matches the field observation.

3.5  Because the accumulator is seeded from the real-time clock at boot, a
     power cycle resets the error to zero. This is why the drift correlates
     with uptime and why sites that power down nightly saw a maximum error of
     a few minutes and largely did not notice.

3.6  The affected fields and the magnitude of the drift in each:

       MSH-7   assembled at send time              full drift
       OBR-7   specimen collected at, or, where    full drift where the
               no order exists, aspiration time     instrument supplies it;
                                                    UNAFFECTED where the
                                                    collection time came from
                                                    the order
       OBR-22  result reported at                  full drift
       OBX-14  observation made at                 full drift

     The variation in OBR-7 behaviour explains why some sites saw the problem
     in OBR-7 and others did not: sites whose LIS supplies a collection time
     with the order were passing that value through unaltered.

3.7  Not affected: the on-screen timestamp, the printed report, the instrument
     audit log, and the VeriConnect-uploaded telemetry timestamp. All of these
     read the RTC directly. This asymmetry is what made the defect visible to
     customers as a mismatch between the instrument display and the LIS, and
     is also why the instrument's own logs were of no help in diagnosing it.

3.8  Analyte values are computed from the optical and impedance measurements
     and have no dependency on the timestamp path. No result value was
     altered. This was verified by re-analysis of 500 archived results from an
     affected instrument against their original transmitted values.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY VERIFICATION DID NOT CATCH IT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  The automated HL7 conformance suite runs 1,200 messages against a
     freshly booted instrument and completes in 38 minutes. Maximum
     accumulated drift at the end of the run on a Pro is approximately 8
     seconds. The suite asserts that timestamps are well-formed, are in the
     correct time zone, and are monotonically non-decreasing across the run.
     All three assertions pass at 8 seconds of drift.

4.2  There was no assertion comparing the instrument's emitted timestamp to
     an independent time source.

4.3  The soak test that would have caught it - a 14-day continuous run - is
     part of the release process for major versions only. 4.2.1 was a hotfix
     for an unrelated RFID read reliability problem (E-302) and went through
     the abbreviated hotfix path.

4.4  The refactor was not flagged as risky in review because the reviewer
     understood the change as an optimisation of how the same value was read,
     not as a change to what value was read. The commit message described it
     as "avoid I2C lock contention in HL7 assembly", which is accurate and
     entirely misses the point.

5. IMPACT ASSESSMENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Instruments on 4.2.1 or 4.2.2 at any time: 604 across 88 accounts.
     Of these, 197 are VeriScan 400 Pro.

5.2  Instruments with more than 7 days of continuous uptime while on affected
     firmware, and therefore with a drift likely to be noticeable: 141, of
     which 118 are Pro.

5.3  Reported operational impacts:
       - QC chart misordering in VeriConnect (all Pro sites with Professional
         tier or above).
       - LIS turnaround time reporting invalid (11 sites).
       - Middleware message rejection where the receiving system validates
         OBR-7 against its own clock (4 sites). Presented to users as E-503.
       - Delta check anomalies where the LIS orders by OBR-7 (2 sites).

5.4  Clinical risk assessment (QA-HHE-2025-004, 2025-03-06): residual risk
     LOW. Result values are unaffected. The credible harm pathway is a
     clinician misjudging the recency of a result, which requires the
     clinician to be reading the timestamp, the drift to be large enough to
     matter, and the difference to be clinically material. In haematology the
     window in which a CBC's exact minute of collection changes management is
     narrow. No adverse outcome was reported.

5.5  Reportability assessed under SOP-VBS-014 section 7. Determined not
     reportable as an incident; the corrective action was communicated as a
     technical bulletin (VBB-0041) rather than a field safety notice. The
     rationale is recorded in the complaint register.

     NOTE. This determination was queried by one customer's regulatory lead
     (see VBST-3925) on the grounds that a wrong timestamp on a clinical
     record is a data integrity defect regardless of the value being correct.
     Regulatory Affairs reviewed and maintained the determination, on the
     basis that the criterion is potential for harm rather than data
     correctness in itself. The exchange is on file.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE 4.2.2 EPISODE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  4.2.2 was assembled and released within 4 days of the defect being
     reproduced. It restored the direct RTC read for MSH-7 only, on the
     mistaken understanding that MSH-7 was the field customers were
     complaining about.

6.2  It also introduced a comparison between the accumulator and the RTC that
     raised E-507 (internal clock not synchronised) whenever they diverged -
     which, since the accumulator was still drifting for the other three
     fields, was constantly.

6.3  4.2.2 was withdrawn after 4 days. 23 instruments received it.

6.4  The 4.2.2 episode is the more instructive failure. The team responded to
     the pressure of a live customer problem by shipping a fix that had been
     scoped from the ticket summaries rather than from the defect. The
     abbreviated hotfix path that let 4.2.1 through also let 4.2.2 through.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. CORRECTIVE AND PREVENTIVE ACTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  CAPA-2025-021  Firmware 4.2.3: restore direct RTC read for all four
                 timestamp fields; resynchronise on every result
                 transmission; report drift telemetry to VeriConnect daily.
                 Status: complete, released 2025-03-19.

  CAPA-2025-022  Add an absolute time assertion to the HL7 conformance suite,
                 comparing emitted timestamps against an independent NTP
                 reference with a tolerance of 2 seconds.
                 Status: complete 2025-04-11.

  CAPA-2025-023  Add a 72-hour continuous-uptime soak with timestamp
                 assertion to the hotfix release path, not only to the major
                 version path. Accepts a 3-day delay on hotfixes.
                 Status: complete 2025-04-30.

  CAPA-2025-024  Code review checklist: any change touching a time source, a
                 clock read or a monotonic counter is flagged for a second
                 reviewer and requires an explicit statement of what the
                 change means, not only what it does.
                 Status: complete 2025-04-18.

  CAPA-2025-025  VeriConnect to surface instrument clock drift on the fleet
                 dashboard and alert above 60 seconds.
                 Status: complete, VeriConnect 6.1, 2025-06-24.

  CAPA-2025-026  QC chart annotation for firmware changes, so that a
                 discontinuity in a chart can be attributed.
                 Status: delivered VeriConnect 6.2, 2026-04-21.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. OBSERVATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  The defect was in the field for 16 days before the first ticket and 21
     days before the two tickets were linked. Both original tickets were
     triaged to the wrong component - one to VeriConnect charting, one to LIS
     integration - because that is where the symptom appeared. Neither
     mentioned firmware, because from the customer's position the firmware was
     not the thing that had changed; it had updated itself weeks earlier
     without incident.

8.2  A first-line question of the form "what changed on this instrument in the
     last 30 days" would have surfaced the firmware update in both cases. It
     is now the second question on the L2 triage template for any complaint
     whose symptom involves data appearing wrong in a downstream system.

8.3  The performance optimisation that caused this saved 5.4 ms per message.
     At a site running 400 messages a day this is 2.2 seconds a day.
