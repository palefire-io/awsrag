James,

I am writing formally on behalf of the Greater Manchester Pathology Network
regarding Field Safety Notice FSN-2025-003.

I want to start by acknowledging that your technical people have been good
throughout. Marcus Chen in particular has been straight with us and has not
tried to minimise anything. This email is not about them.

It is about the operational consequences of the notice landing on a network of
our size, and about two things I think Veridia has got wrong.

THE SCALE OF WHAT YOU HAVE ASKED US TO DO

We operate fourteen instruments across six sites. Between us we had affected
stock at four of those sites. The notice requires a retrospective review of WBC
results reported while affected reagent was on board.

Working out our exposure took my team eleven days. Not because the work is
hard, but because the information needed to do it is scattered across the
instruments, the LIS and the reagent goods-in records, and none of those
systems talks to the others in a way that lets you ask "which samples ran while
cartridge X was on instrument Y". We ended up reconciling by hand.

The final figure is 9,412 patient episodes across the network. Our consultants
have reviewed the flagged subset and are working through the unflagged results
with an elevated WBC. To date nothing clinically significant has emerged, which
is a relief but is not the same as it having been costless.

I estimate the direct cost to us at somewhere between 180 and 220 scientist
hours, plus consultant time.

THE TWO THINGS I THINK YOU GOT WRONG

First, the notice was hard to apply to a mixed fleet. It says the VeriScan 400
Pro is not affected, and it says the affected lots must be withdrawn from all
instruments. Those two statements sit in different sections and several of my
site leads read the first and stopped. One site quarantined two Pro instruments
unnecessarily and lost a day of capacity. Another continued using affected
stock on a VeriScan 400 for three days after the notice because the person who
read it had a Pro in front of them. Both of those are on us to some degree, but
a notice that produces opposite errors at two sites in the same network is a
notice that could be written more clearly.

Second, and more importantly: you had no way for us to answer the retrospective
question. You have built a cloud platform that knows which instrument ran which
sample and which cartridge was on board at the time. It should have been able
to produce that list in a minute. Instead we did it with spreadsheets.

WHAT WE NEED

1. A written summary of the investigation findings when VBI-0087 closes,
   suitable for our clinical governance committee. Not the full report if that
   is commercially sensitive, but enough that the committee can see the root
   cause was identified and addressed.

2. A commitment, with a date, on a retrospective reagent lot exposure report in
   VeriConnect. We are an Enterprise customer with VeriGuard. If this
   capability does not exist for us it does not exist for anyone.

3. A conversation about the cost. I am not sending you an invoice and I am not
   threatening anything. But we have absorbed a substantial amount of work
   caused by a defect in your product, and the free replacement reagent does
   not touch it. I would like that acknowledged in whatever form Veridia thinks
   appropriate.

4. Clearer scope statements in future field safety notices. Say what is NOT
   affected as prominently as what is.

I would like to discuss this rather than exchange letters. I am available most
of next week.
