We have been informed by your support team that firmware 4.2.1 causes result

My question is why this is being handled as a technical bulletin rather than

A timestamp on a clinical record is part of the record. If it is wrong, the
record is wrong. I would like to understand your reasoning, and I would like it
in writing, because I will have to justify our position to our own governance

[2025-03-03 14:50] Sarah Lindgren (Regulatory Affairs Manager):
Ms Marsh, thank you for putting this directly. I would rather answer a
challenge like this properly than have it go unasked.

Our reasoning, and I will set it out fully so you can test it.

The reportability criterion under the UK MDR and under IVDR Article 82 is
whether the event has led, might have led, or might lead to death or serious
deterioration in health. It is a harm criterion, not a correctness criterion.
A defect can produce an incorrect record without meeting it.

Our assessment (documented in health hazard evaluation QA-HHE-2025-004,
6 March):

1. Analyte values are unaffected. The measurement path has no dependency on
   the timestamp assembly. We verified this by re-analysing 500 archived
   results from an affected instrument against their transmitted values;
   all identical.

2. The error is always forward - a result appears more recent than it is,
   never older. The failure mode is therefore not "a clinician believes a
   stale result is current", which would be the concerning direction.

3. Where the LIS supplies a collection time with the order, OBR-7 passes
   through unaltered and is not affected. Only instrument-supplied times
   drift.

4. The magnitude is bounded by instrument uptime and resets on power cycle.

5. For the harm pathway to be realised, a clinician would need to be relying
   on the precise minute of a full blood count's collection or reporting time
   to make a decision that would differ if the time were tens of minutes
   earlier. We could not construct a credible haematology scenario in which
   that is true. In a different discipline - a troponin series, say, where
   timing between samples drives interpretation - our answer might well be
   different.

On that basis the assessment was "not reportable as an incident", and the
corrective action is being communicated as a technical bulletin with a
mandatory update recommendation. The rationale is recorded in our complaint
register whether or not we report, which is itself a requirement.

To answer your direct question: it has not been reported to the MHRA as an
incident. The MHRA has not been notified of a field safety corrective action
because we have not determined one to be necessary.

I will send you this in a formal letter on headed paper, and I am content for
you to share it with UKAS or your governance committee.

Two further things:
- I would encourage you to disagree with us if you do. If your assessment is
  that this is reportable from your side as a user, you are entitled to report
  it to the MHRA independently and we will not object or obstruct.
- The bulletin (VBB-0041) will state the affected fields, the mechanism and
  the bounds, so you have the technical basis to make your own judgement.
