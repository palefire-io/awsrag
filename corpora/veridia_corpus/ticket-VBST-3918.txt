[2025-02-27 09:15] Gavin Tuck (Firmware Lead):
Dr Ruettimann, Gavin Tuck, firmware lead. Your question deserves an answer
from me rather than from support.

4.2.2 went through our hotfix release path, which is abbreviated compared with
the path for a major version: it omits the extended soak test. That is the same
path 4.2.1 took, and it is the reason both defects reached you.

The abbreviated path exists because a hotfix is, in principle, a small and
well-understood change. In this case the change was neither, because we scoped
it from customer ticket summaries rather than from the defect analysis, which
was not complete when we shipped.

What we are changing, and I will put this in writing formally once the
investigation closes:
- a 72-hour continuous-uptime soak with an independent time reference is being
  added to the hotfix path, not just the major version path;
- no hotfix ships before the investigation identifying the defect is complete;
- any change touching a time source gets a second reviewer.

The device's conformity assessment is not affected by a firmware withdrawal as
such - software changes are managed under our change control and the technical
documentation is updated accordingly - but I would not want to leave you with
the impression that this was a good process. It was not.

4.2.2 reached 23 instruments. Yours is one of them. All 23 have been contacted
directly.

--- Resolution ---
Root cause: firmware 4.2.2, an incomplete fix for VBI-0091, which corrected
MSH-7 only and introduced spurious E-507 warnings. Withdrawn 2025-03-03.
Actions: rollback to 4.2.0; subsequent update to 4.2.3.
Process failure acknowledged to the customer in writing by the firmware lead.
See also: VBI-0091 section 6, VBB-0041.
