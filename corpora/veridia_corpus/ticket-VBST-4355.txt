[2026-02-11 13:00] Marcus Chen (QC Applications Specialist):
Helen, Marcus Chen from Applications. Owen has passed this to me because the
"is there any way" question deserves a proper answer rather than just "no".

The direct answer is no - and I want to be emphatic about it, because this is
somewhere laboratories occasionally improvise and shouldn't.

Do NOT run body fluids in whole blood mode on a VeriScan 400. It will produce
numbers. The numbers will be wrong, and wrong in a way that is not obvious:
the gating, the dilution and the lyse step are all wrong for a low-cellularity
fluid, the background is far too high relative to the counts you are looking
for, and there is no carryover protection appropriate to the sample type. A
CSF white cell count is a result on which somebody decides whether to start
antibiotics. It is one of the few haematology numbers where being wrong is
immediately dangerous.

Your options are:
1. Manual chamber count. Still the reference method and entirely appropriate
   for your likely volume.
2. Refer to a laboratory with a Pro. Your network may already have one.
3. A VeriScan 400 Pro. That's a conversation with Peter Doyle, and if CSF
   volume is low it may not be justifiable - I'd rather tell you that than
   have you buy an instrument for six samples a week.

Happy to talk through the validation requirements for any of these if useful.

--- Resolution ---
No fault. Customer operating a VeriScan 400 believed it was a 400 Pro, having
been supplied the Pro datasheet in error in their August 2025 installation
pack. Body fluid mode does not exist on the VeriScan 400.
Actions: correct documentation supplied (DS-VS400-003 and current IFU); Pro
datasheet removed from the account document set; installation pack assembly
process raised with the installation team.
Customer strongly advised against running body fluids in whole blood mode;
manual chamber count recommended for their volume.
See also: VBST-4142, VBST-4175, spec-veriscan-400-pro-v3.txt
