1. SUMMARY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Twenty-one production lots of VeriLyse RGT-L3 lyse reagent, formulation
revision C, manufactured between 2025-01-27 and 2025-02-11, develop an
elevated baseline absorbance at 535 nm during on-board use. The elevation
becomes measurable from approximately day 5 after first pierce and continues
to increase for the remainder of the on-board period.

On VeriScan 400 instruments, which use a halogen source and a single-beam
absorbance measurement for the WBC differential, the elevation shifts the
gating thresholds and produces spurious abnormal-scatter flags, W-042
warnings, E-208 errors and a positive WBC bias of up to +0.9 x10^9/L. On
VeriScan 400 Pro instruments, which use a laser source with a reference
photodiode and per-run baseline subtraction, no clinically significant
deviation was demonstrable.

Root cause was established as catalytic oxidation of the revision C organic
buffer by trace copper introduced with a single raw material delivery, itself
the consequence of an undeclared change of intermediate supplier by the raw
material vendor.

Corrective action: recall of the affected lots (FSN-2025-003); reformulation
to revision D with a dual buffer system substantially less sensitive to trace
metal contamination (VBB-0047); addition of an incoming trace metal
specification and test; contractual change notification obligation imposed on
the raw material vendor.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. BACKGROUND AND TRIGGER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  The first complaint attributable to this issue was VBST-3847, received
     2025-01-28 from Countess of Chester Hospital NHS Foundation Trust
     (ACC-1090), reporting an increase in WBC abnormal-scatter flags on a
     VeriScan 400 (serial VS4-0402) without a corresponding change in the
     patient population.

2.2  Four further complaints with a similar presentation were received by
     2025-02-03 (VBST-3851, VBST-3863, VBST-3871, VBST-3884). The weekly
     clustering review on 2025-02-03 identified the pattern; at that point
     three of the five complaints shared an RGT-L3 lot within a narrow range.

2.3  This investigation was opened 2025-02-04. The health hazard evaluation
     was completed 2025-02-12 and FSN-2025-003 was issued 2025-02-14.

2.4  Observation. The interval from first complaint to field safety notice was
     17 days. Two of the five complaints had been closed locally as
     "instrument fault - no fault found" before the pattern was recognised.
     See section 9.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. INVESTIGATION - SITE DATA
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Field service attended three sites (FSV-2025-0146, FSV-2025-0149,
     FSV-2025-0155). In each case the instrument passed optical calibration,
     background counts were normal immediately after an Extended Clean, and no
     hardware fault was found.

3.2  In each case the elevated background returned within 24 to 72 hours of
     the clean, and in each case swapping the RGT-L3 cartridge for one from an
     earlier lot resolved the problem immediately.

3.3  The correlation with cartridge age on board was noted at
     FSV-2025-0155 (Betsi Cadwaladr, 2025-02-18) by the attending engineer,
     who recorded that the site's two instruments were both affected but the
     one whose cartridge had been fitted four days earlier was markedly less
     affected than the one fitted eleven days earlier. This observation
     directed the laboratory investigation to on-board ageing rather than to
     as-manufactured quality, which is why the release testing data had not
     flagged the lots.

3.4  Nineteen complaints in total were ultimately attributed to this issue,
     from 11 accounts, all on VeriScan 400 instruments.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. INVESTIGATION - LABORATORY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Retained samples of lots L3-2025-0433 (unaffected), L3-2025-0444
     (affected), L3-2025-0452 (affected) and L3-2025-0463 (adjacent, later
     quarantined) were tested.

4.2  Absorbance at 535 nm, measured against a water blank, as received:

       Lot              t=0 (AU)   Release spec
       ---------------  ---------  ------------
       L3-2025-0433     0.008      <= 0.015
       L3-2025-0444     0.011      <= 0.015
       L3-2025-0452     0.012      <= 0.015
       L3-2025-0463     0.011      <= 0.015

     All four lots met the release specification as manufactured. This is the
     central point of the investigation: the defect is not detectable at
     release.

4.3  Accelerated ageing, 35 C, absorbance at 535 nm:

       Days    0433    0444    0452    0463
       -----   -----   -----   -----   -----
       0       0.008   0.011   0.012   0.011
       7       0.009   0.024   0.026   0.019
       14      0.009   0.038   0.041   0.027
       28      0.010   0.055   0.058   0.034
       56      0.012   0.068   0.071   0.041
       84      0.014   0.076   0.079   0.048

     Specification at end of on-board life is <= 0.025 AU. Lots 0444 and 0452
     exceed it by day 7 at 35 C. Lot 0463 exceeds it between days 14 and 28
     and was quarantined on this basis, although no field complaint was
     attributed to it.

4.4  Real-time ageing at 22 C on instrument reproduced the effect at
     approximately one third the rate, consistent with the field observation
     that the problem becomes apparent from around day 5 to day 10 in a
     laboratory at typical ambient.

4.5  UV-visible scanning of aged affected material showed a broad absorbance
     band centred at 531 nm not present in the unaffected lot. LC-MS
     identified the species as an oxidation product of the revision C organic
     buffer.

4.6  ICP-MS of the four lots:

       Lot              Cu (ppb)   Fe (ppb)   Mn (ppb)
       ---------------  ---------  ---------  --------
       L3-2025-0433     < 1        14         < 1
       L3-2025-0444     41         16         < 1
       L3-2025-0452     46         15         < 1
       L3-2025-0463     22         14         < 1

     Copper at 40-50 ppb in the affected lots against < 1 ppb in the
     unaffected lot. Lot 0463 is intermediate, consistent with its position at
     the end of the affected raw material campaign.

4.7  Spiking experiment: unaffected lot L3-2025-0433 spiked with copper(II) at
     50 ppb and aged at 35 C reproduced the absorbance profile of the affected
     lots within experimental error (day 28: 0.052 AU spiked, 0.055 and 0.058
     AU for the affected lots). This is taken as confirmation of the mechanism.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. INVESTIGATION - MANUFACTURING AND SUPPLY CHAIN
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  All 21 affected lots were manufactured from a single delivery of the
     organic buffering agent, vendor lot BV-24-11-338, received 2025-01-22.

5.2  Lots manufactured before 2025-01-27 used the previous delivery
     (BV-24-09-201); lots from L3-2025-0471 onward used delivery BV-25-02-107
     and are unaffected, although by then the formulation change was already
     in progress.

5.3  ICP-MS of retained samples of the three vendor deliveries:

       BV-24-09-201    Cu < 1 ppb
       BV-24-11-338    Cu 780 ppb
       BV-25-02-107    Cu 3 ppb

5.4  The vendor's certificate of analysis for BV-24-11-338 reported
     conformance on all specified parameters. Trace metals were not a
     specified parameter on either Veridia's incoming specification or the
     vendor's CoA. Neither party was testing for it.

5.5  Supplier audit conducted 2025-03-06 established that the vendor had
     changed the supplier of a synthesis intermediate in October 2024. The new
     intermediate is produced using a copper-catalysed coupling step. The
     vendor did not notify Veridia of the change because Veridia's supply
     agreement required notification of changes to the specified parameters
     only, and the change did not affect them.

5.6  This is a supplier control failure on Veridia's part as much as a
     notification failure on the vendor's. The supply agreement was drafted
     around a specification that did not contemplate a catalytic sensitivity
     that Veridia had not characterised.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHY THE VERISCAN 400 PRO IS NOT AFFECTED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  240 residual clinical specimens were analysed across 4 VeriScan 400 Pro
     instruments using affected lot L3-2025-0452 aged to 28 days on board,
     paired against the same specimens on unaffected lot L3-2025-0433.

6.2  Results:

       Analyte   Slope    Intercept   Mean bias        95% LoA
       -------   ------   ---------   --------------   ------------------
       WBC       1.004    +0.03       +0.06 x10^9/L    -0.19 to +0.31
       NEUT%     0.999    +0.11       +0.10 pp         -0.9 to +1.1
       LYMPH%    1.002    -0.09       -0.05 pp         -0.8 to +0.7
       RBC       1.000    0.00        0.00             -0.04 to +0.04
       HGB       1.001    -0.2 g/L    -0.1 g/L         -1.4 to +1.2
       PLT       0.998    +1.1        +0.6 x10^9/L     -5.2 to +6.4

6.3  No result met the criteria for clinical significance. Flag rates were
     equivalent (affected 4.2 per cent, unaffected 3.9 per cent, n.s.).

6.4  Mechanism: the Pro's optical bench measures a reference beam through a
     path containing the same reagent and subtracts it per run. An absorbance
     offset common to sample and reference cancels. The VeriScan 400's
     single-beam arrangement has no such compensation and relies on a baseline
     established at calibration, which the drift invalidates.

6.5  The same 240-specimen comparison on a VeriScan 400 produced a mean WBC
     bias of +0.41 x10^9/L with a 95th percentile of +0.87, and a flag rate of
     14.1 per cent against 3.8 per cent on unaffected reagent.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. RISK AND CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  The health hazard evaluation (QA-HHE-2025-002, 2025-02-12) concluded that
     the residual risk is LOW, principally because:
     a) the great majority of affected results carry an instrument flag;
     b) laboratory practice requires flagged differentials to be reviewed;
     c) the bias is positive and in the WBC channel only, so the failure mode
        is an unnecessary review or repeat rather than a missed abnormality;
     d) haemoglobin, red cell and platelet parameters are unaffected.

7.2  The evaluation identified as the worst credible case a mildly elevated
     WBC in an otherwise normal sample being reported without review and
     prompting an unnecessary investigation. No such case was reported.

7.3  No adverse patient outcome, serious deterioration or death was reported
     to Veridia in connection with this issue. Reportability was assessed
     under SOP-VBS-014 section 7; the FSCA itself was notified to competent
     authorities.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. CORRECTIVE AND PREVENTIVE ACTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  CAPA-2025-011  Recall affected lots. FSN-2025-003 issued 2025-02-14.
                 Status: complete. 94 per cent of shipped affected units
                 accounted for by 2025-03-31.

  CAPA-2025-012  Reformulate RGT-L3 to a dual buffer system with reduced
                 sensitivity to trace metal catalysis. Revision D, first
                 production lot L3-2025-0471. VBB-0047 issued 2025-03-31.
                 Status: complete.

  CAPA-2025-013  Add trace metals (Cu, Fe, Mn, Zn) to the incoming
                 specification for the buffering agent, with ICP-MS testing on
                 every delivery. Cu limit 10 ppb.
                 Status: complete, effective 2025-03-10.

  CAPA-2025-014  Amend the supply agreement to require notification of any
                 change to raw materials, intermediates, synthesis route or
                 manufacturing site, irrespective of whether specified
                 parameters are affected. Extend to all critical raw material
                 vendors, not only this one.
                 Status: complete for this vendor 2025-04-01; extension to the
                 remaining seven critical vendors tracked separately under
                 CAPA-2025-019.

  CAPA-2025-015  Add an accelerated stability check (35 C, 28 days,
                 absorbance at 535 nm) to lot release for RGT-L3. Note this
                 delays release by 28 days; mitigated by holding a larger
                 finished goods buffer stock.
                 Status: complete, effective 2025-05-01.

  CAPA-2025-016  Amend SOP-VBS-001 to require verification of reagent lot
                 numbers against active field safety notices as part of daily
                 QC. Status: complete, SOP-VBS-001 revision 6.

  CAPA-2025-017  Amend SOP-VBS-014 to introduce weekly complaint clustering
                 review and to reduce the health hazard evaluation target from
                 15 to 5 working days. Status: complete, revision 2.

  CAPA-2025-018  Build a retrospective reagent lot exposure report into
                 VeriConnect so that customers can identify affected patient
                 episodes without manual reconciliation.
                 Status: delivered in VeriConnect 6.1 beta, general
                 availability 6.2 (2026-04-21). Instrument-side export
                 delivered in firmware 4.4.0.

9. OBSERVATIONS AND LESSONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
9.1  Release testing measured the correct parameter at the wrong time. The
     specification was written around as-manufactured absorbance; the failure
     mode was a rate of change during use. A specification that cannot detect
     the failure mode is not a control.

9.2  Two early complaints were closed as instrument faults. In both cases the
     instrument passed every test applied to it, which is exactly what one
     would expect when the instrument is not the problem. The absence of an
     instrument fault should have prompted a reagent hypothesis sooner.

9.3  The distinction between "no fault found" and "cannot reproduce" matters.
     Both early complaints were recorded as the former when the latter was
     accurate. SOP-VBS-014 revision 2 section 6.4 now addresses this.

9.4  The engineer observation at FSV-2025-0155 correlating severity with days
     on board was the single most useful piece of evidence in the
     investigation and it came from someone writing down something they
     noticed that nobody had asked them to record.

9.5  Customers with mixed VeriScan 400 and 400 Pro fleets found the scope of
     the notice hard to apply. Several quarantined Pro instruments
     unnecessarily; one continued using affected stock on a 400 believing the
     notice applied only to the Pro. FSN scope statements must state what is
     NOT affected as prominently as what is.
