[2025-02-03 11:20] Marcus Chen (QC Applications Specialist):
Eleri, Marcus Chen from Applications. Hannah has looped me in.

What we understand at this point: the effect is a positive bias on the WBC
count, on the order of a few tenths up to about 0.9 x10^9/L at the extreme,
plus an increase in false abnormal-scatter flags. RBC, HGB and platelets are
not affected. Most affected results flag, so they should have reached a
scientist for review before authorisation.

Your instrument went to a hard E-208 stop, which means it detected the drift
and refused to produce results rather than producing biased ones. That's the
protective behaviour working. The results that concern us are the ones from
the days before the stop, when the drift was present but below the error
threshold.

Your exposure window is 26 January to 3 February on VS4-0655.

I would tell your head of department: a reagent problem is under
investigation, the failure mode is a positive WBC bias with increased
flagging, red cells and haemoglobin are unaffected, and a retrospective review
of WBC results from that window is prudent. I'd rather you said that than
something more reassuring that I then have to correct.
