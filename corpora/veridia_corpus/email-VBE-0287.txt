Dear Dr Marchetti,

Thank you for your presentation at the Diagnostics Forum in Vienna last week.
"What our release specification could not see" was, by some distance, the most
discussed session of the meeting, and I have had a dozen people ask me for your
slides.

I am writing with three things.

First, a request. Would you be willing to share the slides, or a version of
them? Several attendees asked and I would rather point them to something you
have approved than to their own photographs of the screen.

Second, an invitation. We are planning next year's programme and I would like
to invite you to give an extended version, perhaps 40 minutes, as part of a
session on post-market surveillance. What made your talk land was that you
described a failure in your own product in specific detail, including the parts
that reflected badly on your company. That is rare and the room noticed. I
would like more of it and I think you are the person to do it.

Third, a question of my own, which you may prefer not to answer publicly. You
said the release specification measured absorbance at manufacture and the
failure mode was a rate of change during use, and that "a specification that
cannot detect the failure mode is not a control". I have been turning that over
since. Do you think this is a general problem in IVD reagent release testing,
or was it specific to the chemistry involved? I ask because if it is general
then a lot of us are testing the wrong thing.

I should say that two of our committee members were initially uneasy about a
manufacturer speaking on post-market surveillance, on the grounds that it might
become a sales pitch. Your talk settled that. It would help me if the extended
version were similarly free of product names beyond what the story requires.

With warm regards,
