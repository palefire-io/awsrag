1. SUMMARY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Three material discrepancies were identified in the candidate's employment
history, professional registration and qualifications. Each was put to the
candidate with the evidence and he was given a full opportunity to respond.
His responses did not resolve the discrepancies and, in one instance,
introduced a further inconsistency.

The offer was withdrawn with Chief People Officer approval on 2025-07-21. The
candidate was in employment throughout and had not resigned.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT THE CANDIDATE CLAIMED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
From his CV and application, and repeated at interview on 2025-05-14:

  2.1  Senior Regulatory Affairs Manager, Calderfield Diagnostics Ltd,
       March 2021 to present.
  2.2  Regulatory Affairs Manager, Northmoor Medical Devices Ltd,
       June 2016 to February 2021.
  2.3  MSc Regulatory Affairs, University of Hertfordshire, 2015.
  2.4  "TOPRA registered" - stated on the CV under professional membership.
  2.5  At interview, described leading the IVDR transition submission for a
       Class C device at Calderfield, which was the single most relevant piece
       of experience for our role and was material to the decision to offer.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT SCREENING FOUND
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  CALDERFIELD DIAGNOSTICS LTD. Confirmed employment, but as Regulatory
     Affairs Officer from 2021-03-08 to 2023-11-30, and as Senior Regulatory
     Affairs Officer from 2023-12-01. NOT Senior Regulatory Affairs Manager,
     and the employment was continuing, so "to present" was accurate.

     Calderfield HR confirmed in writing on 2025-06-19 that they hold no
     record of the candidate having held a manager-grade role.

3.2  NORTHMOOR MEDICAL DEVICES LTD. The company was dissolved in 2019.
     Companies House record: company number 09441207, incorporated 2015-01-14,
     dissolved via compulsory strike-off 2019-08-06. The claimed employment
     ran to February 2021, which is 18 months after the company ceased to
     exist.

3.3  MSc REGULATORY AFFAIRS, UNIVERSITY OF HERTFORDSHIRE, 2015. The university
     confirmed on 2025-06-27 that it holds no record of the candidate
     completing the programme. It confirmed he had ENROLLED on the MSc
     Regulatory Affairs programme in September 2013 and had exited with a
     Postgraduate Certificate in 2014. A PGCert is a genuine and respectable
     qualification. It is not an MSc.

3.4  TOPRA REGISTRATION. TOPRA (The Organisation for Professionals in
     Regulatory Affairs) confirmed no current or lapsed membership in that
     name.

3.5  REFERENCES. Two provided. The Calderfield reference was returned and was
     satisfactory in content but described his role as Senior Regulatory
     Affairs Officer, consistent with 3.1. The second referee, given as a
     former line manager at Northmoor, could not be contacted - the email
     address bounced and the mobile number was unobtainable. Vetted Assurance
     could find no professional footprint for the named individual.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. PUTTING IT TO THE CANDIDATE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Tobias Frankl wrote to the candidate on 2025-07-01 setting out each
     finding with the evidence, and offering a call. This is the standard
     process and is not optional.

4.2  Call held 2025-07-04. Present: Adrian Mowbray-Cass, Tobias Frankl,
     Rachel Duffy (note taker). The candidate was told at the outset that the
     purpose was to hear his account and that no decision had been taken.

4.3  His responses:

     On the job title: he said that "Senior Regulatory Affairs Officer and
     Senior Regulatory Affairs Manager are the same job in most companies" and
     that he had used the title he thought we would recognise. He accepted the
     formal title was Officer.

     On Northmoor: he said the dissolution date was a Companies House error and
     that the business "continued trading informally" after strike-off, with
     him continuing to be paid. Asked whether he could provide payslips, a P60
     or an employment contract for the period after August 2019, he said he
     would look. Nothing was subsequently provided.

     On the MSc: he said he had "completed the taught element" and had always
     intended to submit the dissertation. Asked directly whether he held an
     MSc, he said no. Asked why the CV said MSc, he said it was "shorthand".

     On TOPRA: he said the membership had lapsed "years ago" and that he had
     not updated the CV. TOPRA confirmed no record of membership ever having
     existed. This was put to him. He said TOPRA's records must be wrong.

     On the IVDR submission: he said he had "worked on" the submission as part
     of a team. Asked whether he had led it, he said he had led "elements".
     The Calderfield reference, which named the person who led that submission,
     names someone else.

4.4  Rachel Duffy's contemporaneous note: "He was not evasive and he was not
     hostile. He answered every question. The difficulty is that each answer
     reframed the original claim as something narrower, and by the end of the
     call none of the five claims stood as originally made. When I put that to
     him he said 'everyone rounds up on a CV'."

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. ASSESSMENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Considered individually, item 3.1 alone might have been survivable. Title
     inflation is common and the underlying experience was real.

5.2  It is the pattern that is decisive. Five claims, of which one is
     unverifiable and four are demonstrably inaccurate in the same direction.

5.3  MATERIALITY TO THIS ROLE SPECIFICALLY. This is a Regulatory Affairs
     appointment at L4. The postholder would sign submissions to a Notified
     Body, would represent Veridia to the MHRA and to Swissmedic, and would
     make reportability determinations under the vigilance system. Sarah
     Lindgren's assessment, provided in writing on 2025-07-09: "The whole
     function rests on being believed. If I put someone in front of BSI whose
     own CV overstates their qualifications, and it emerges later, it does not
     just end that person. It calls into question every document with their
     name on it."

5.4  Sunita Raval's advice, 2025-07-11: the offer is conditional on
     satisfactory screening, the condition has not been met, and withdrawal is
     contractually straightforward. She advised that the withdrawal letter
     state the factual findings and avoid any characterisation of intent -
     Veridia can say what it found and that it does not meet the condition,
     without asserting dishonesty. She also noted the candidate remained in
     employment and had not resigned, materially reducing any reliance
     argument.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. DECISION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  Offer withdrawn. Approved by Naomi Okonjo-Bright, 2025-07-18. Letter
     issued 2025-07-21.

6.2  The letter set out each of the four verifiable findings, stated that the
     conditional offer was withdrawn because the screening condition had not
     been satisfied, and offered a right to respond within 14 days.

6.3  The candidate responded on 2025-07-29, disputing the TOPRA finding only
     and accepting the others. Naomi Okonjo-Bright reviewed and upheld the
     decision on 2025-08-05. No further contact.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. LEARNING
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  The claimed IVDR experience was the single most influential factor in the
     hiring decision and was not verifiable by any check in our standard
     screening. It emerged only because the reference happened to name the
     person who actually led the work.

     RECOMMENDATION, adopted: for L4 and above in regulated functions, the
     reference request now asks the referee to confirm the candidate's role on
     the specific projects the candidate cited at interview, by name. Adopted
     into the screening guide at version 5.

7.2  Northmoor's dissolution was discoverable in ninety seconds on Companies
     House and would have been found before interview if anyone had looked.
     Recommendation, adopted: for L4 and above, a Companies House existence
     check on all claimed employers is run at shortlist stage rather than at
     offer stage.

7.3  Timing. The offer was made on 30 May and withdrawn on 21 July - seven
     weeks. That is too long to leave a candidate in limbo, and we were
     fortunate he had not resigned. Recommendation, adopted: where a Grade C
     finding arises, the candidate is contacted within 5 working days and
     asked directly whether they have given notice.

7.4  Sarah Lindgren asked whether Veridia should tell Calderfield Diagnostics
     that their employee had misrepresented his role. Sunita Raval's advice
     was no: we have no duty to do so, the information came to us through a
     screening process with its own lawful basis, and passing it on would be a
     disclosure the candidate did not consent to and could not have
     anticipated. No disclosure was made.
