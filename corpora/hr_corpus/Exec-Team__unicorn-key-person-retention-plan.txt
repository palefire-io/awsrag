1. THE PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  Veridia is acquiring capability, not revenue. The capability lives in
     people. If the people leave in the first eighteen months, we will have
     paid GBP 62M to GBP 68M for an installed base and a factory.

1.2  Narwhal's overall voluntary turnover is 22 per cent. Its R&D turnover is
     8 per cent, and R&D is where the capability sits. That is reassuring
     until you consider that an acquisition is precisely the event that moves
     an 8 per cent population.

1.3  Industry evidence on acquisitions: senior target-company departures run
     at roughly double the base rate in the two years following completion,
     concentrated in months 6 to 18 once earn-outs and initial retention
     periods expire.

5. PRINCIPLES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Retention payments buy time, not commitment. They stop someone leaving in
     month six. They do not stop them leaving in month twenty-five. What stops
     that is the job being good.

5.2  Do not pay retention to someone whose departure you would not actually
     mind. It is expensive and it tells everyone else what you value.

5.3  Do not make a retention offer conditional on silence about the
     transaction. It reads as hush money and would be indefensible if it
     surfaced.

5.4  Tell people the truth about their role in the combined structure before
     they sign a retention agreement. A retention payment obtained by allowing
     someone to believe they will run something they will not is a
     twelve-month problem that becomes a claim.

5.5  The single most effective retention measure in this transaction is not on
     this list. It is harmonising terms upwards quickly and visibly. A Narwhal
     engineer whose pension doubles and who gains family private medical on
     day one has been told something about the acquirer that no announcement
     can convey.

6. THE CPO'S CLOSING NOTE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Every acquisition I have seen fails or succeeds on whether the acquired people
believe the acquirer is better than what they had. Narwhal's people have worse
terms, no career framework, no learning budget, a 22 per cent turnover rate
and a manager with three grievances against him still in post.

We can be better than that easily and quickly, and if we are, most of this
retention budget will turn out to have been unnecessary. If we spend eighteen
months running two sets of terms while we model synergies, we will spend the
money and lose the people anyway.
