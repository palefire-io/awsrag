How to read this. Each level describes what someone at that level typically
does. It is not a checklist and you do not need to satisfy every line. The
question a calibration panel asks is "does the overall shape of this person's
work look like this level", not "have they ticked eleven of fourteen boxes".

-----------------------------------------------------------------
L1 - ASSOCIATE ENGINEER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Scope        Tasks that have been defined for you, with a known approach.
Autonomy     Checks in frequently. Work is reviewed before it lands.
Impact       Your team's output is slightly higher because you are on it.

Typical      Fixes a defect in a component someone else owns. Writes a test.
             Runs a defined diagnostic on a returned instrument. Shadows
             customer visits.
Judgement    Knows when to ask. This is the single most important L1 skill and
             the one that most distinguishes a strong L1 from a struggling one.
Communication Can describe what they did and whether it worked.

Field service specifics: works under supervision. Does not attend a customer
site unaccompanied. Building the diagnostic vocabulary - what E-104 means, what
a background count is, what a Field Safety Notice is.

Moving to L2: you are consistently finishing defined work without needing the
approach handed to you, and people have stopped checking everything.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
L2 - ENGINEER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Scope        Owns defined components or defined customer situations end to
             end. Problems arrive without a solution attached.
Autonomy     Works independently within an area. Raises risks rather than
             discovering them at the end.
Impact       A part of the system works because you look after it.

Typical      Owns a module through design, implementation, test and release.
             Diagnoses an unfamiliar instrument fault to root cause. Handles a
             P2 support escalation without help. Writes the runbook nobody had
             written.
Judgement    Knows what "done" means, including the documentation and the
             regulated records. Recognises when a fix addresses a symptom.
Communication Can explain a technical problem to a customer or to a
             non-engineer without either patronising them or losing them.
Quality      Understands why the quality system exists and works with it
             rather than around it. Knows what a controlled document is.

Field service specifics: attends sites unaccompanied, competency signed off.
Manages their own diary and parts. Writes visit reports that another engineer
could act on twelve months later.

Moving to L3: your judgement in your area has become something other people
rely on, and you are being asked about things outside what you were assigned.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
L3 - SENIOR ENGINEER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Scope        Owns an area - a subsystem, a product surface, a customer region.
             Ambiguous problems. You decide what the problem is, not just how
             to solve it.
Autonomy     Sets the approach. Consulted before decisions in your area are
             made elsewhere.
Impact       An area works, and would visibly degrade if you stopped.

Typical      Owns the fluidics firmware, or the HL7 integration surface, or
             field service for the north of England. Leads the technical
             response to a customer problem that spans teams. Reviews others'
             designs and improves them.
Judgement    Distinguishes the problem worth solving from the problem in front
             of you. Knows when the right answer is "do not build this".
             Understands the regulatory consequence of a technical decision -
             for example that a change to how a result timestamp is derived is
             not merely an optimisation.
Communication Writes things other people read by choice. Can hold a difficult
             conversation with a customer under pressure without escalating it.
Others       Reviews, mentors, and raises the standard of people around them.
             Not necessarily managing anyone.

Field service specifics: the engineer other engineers ring. Handles the
politically difficult sites. Their visit reports change company decisions -
the observation at FSV-2025-0155 that reagent fault severity tracked days on
board redirected an entire investigation.

Moving to L4: your work has started to shape how other teams work, and the
problems you are given have no obvious owner.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
L4 - STAFF ENGINEER (IC TRACK) / HEAD OF (MANAGEMENT TRACK)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Both tracks sit on the same band. Neither is senior to the other.

STAFF ENGINEER
Scope        A system, or a technical problem that crosses teams and has no
             owner. Often the problem nobody has managed to define.
Autonomy     Chooses what to work on, within a direction agreed with a
             Director.
Impact       Multiple teams work differently because of decisions you made.

Typical      Owns the firmware platform across two product lines. Leads the
             technical investigation into a field failure that could become a
             safety notice. Sets the integration architecture. Says the
             uncomfortable thing in the design review that saves six months.
Judgement    Thinks in years and in consequences. Understands the difference
             between a decision that is reversible and one that is not, and
             spends effort accordingly. Can hold "we should ship this" and "we
             are not ready" simultaneously and resolve it honestly.
Influence    Changes what people do without instructing them. This is the
             defining L4 IC skill and it cannot be faked.

HEAD OF
Scope        A department: its people, its work, its budget, its plan.
Autonomy     Accountable for outcomes, not activities.
Impact       A function performs, and its people grow.

Typical      Runs field service engineering across two countries. Owns support
             engineering including the escalation process and its quality.
             Hires, develops, and where necessary exits people.
Judgement    Allocates scarce people to competing demands and defends the
             decision. Distinguishes a struggling person from a person in the
             wrong role from a person being failed by their environment.
People       Gives feedback that lands. Runs honest probation and performance
             conversations. Their people say they have grown.

Moving to L5: you are accountable for a function rather than a department, and
you are shaping what the company does rather than how it does it.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
L5 - DIRECTOR / VP
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Scope        A function. Its strategy, its budget, its structure, its
             leadership bench.
Autonomy     Sets direction within the company plan. Held to outcomes over
             quarters and years.
Impact       The company can do something it otherwise could not.

Typical      Owns customer support globally. Owns engineering. Accountable for
             a function's contribution to the company plan, and for its cost.
Judgement    Makes decisions with incomplete information and owns them
             publicly when they turn out wrong. Balances what a customer needs
             now against what the product needs to become.
Leadership   Builds leaders. Their function keeps working when they are on
             holiday, and that is deliberate.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
THINGS THAT DO NOT MOVE YOU UP A LEVEL
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Stated plainly because these come up in calibration panels every cycle.

  - Time served. Four years at L2 is four years at L2.
  - Being busy. Volume is not scope.
  - Being the only person who understands something, where that is a situation
    you have maintained rather than resolved. Hoarding is not seniority.
  - Working long hours. If anything this is a signal we look at
    uncomfortably, because it often indicates a scoping problem.
  - Being liked, or being difficult. Neither is evidence either way.
  - A competing offer. Pay may be addressed; a level is not.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
A NOTE FOR FIELD SERVICE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Field service progression was, before version 4, badly served by a guide
written around software engineering. The 2024 engagement survey said so
bluntly. This version was written with Priya Anand and three field engineers
and the level descriptors above include field-specific text at every level.

If it still does not describe your job, tell us. Denise Kaur and Priya Anand
would both rather hear it than not.
