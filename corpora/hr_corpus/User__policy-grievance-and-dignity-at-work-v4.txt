1. WHAT THIS COVERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A grievance is a concern about your own treatment at work: your working
conditions, a decision affecting you, the behaviour of a colleague or manager,
pay or terms, bullying, harassment or discrimination.

Concerns about wrongdoing that affect others or the company go through
User__policy-whistleblowing-speak-up-v3.txt. If you are unsure which applies,
raise it either way and we will route it.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. TRY THE INFORMAL ROUTE FIRST, IF YOU CAN
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  Most workplace problems are misunderstandings, thoughtlessness or
     accumulated friction, and most are resolved by a conversation.

2.2  Options: speak to the person directly; ask your manager to help; ask the
     People team to facilitate; ask for mediation.

2.3  MEDIATION. Voluntary, confidential, conducted by a trained mediator
     independent of both parties. It resolves matters faster and better than
     a formal process in most cases. Six of the eleven informal matters raised
     in 2025 went to mediation and five resolved there.

2.4  You are never obliged to approach the person directly, and you should not
     be told to "have a word with them first" if that prospect frightens you.
     Anyone who tells you that has misread this policy.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. RAISING A FORMAL GRIEVANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  In writing to your manager, or to the People team if your grievance
     concerns your manager, or to Naomi Okonjo-Bright if it concerns a member
     of the Executive Committee.

3.2  Set out what happened, when, who was involved, what effect it has had and
     what outcome you are seeking. That last one matters more than people
     expect - a grievance whose desired outcome is unclear is harder to
     resolve.

3.3  You do not need a template, a lawyer or perfect prose.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE PROCESS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Within 5 working days   Acknowledgement and a named hearing manager
  Within 10 working days  Grievance hearing
  Investigation           As long as it genuinely needs, with updates every
                          10 working days
  Within 5 working days
  of conclusion           Written outcome with reasons
  Within 10 working days  Your right of appeal

4.1  The hearing manager is independent of the grievance and is normally more
     senior than anyone it concerns.

4.2  YOU MAY BE ACCOMPANIED at any formal meeting by a colleague or a trade
     union representative. This is a statutory right in the UK and Veridia
     extends it to Swiss employees as a matter of policy. Your companion may
     speak, put your case and confer with you; they may not answer questions
     on your behalf.

4.3  Reasonable adjustments to the process are available - a written process
     instead of a hearing, breaks, a support person in addition to your
     companion, a different venue. Ask.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. APPEALS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Heard by someone more senior than the original hearing manager and not
previously involved. Grounds: new evidence, procedural failure, or that the
outcome was not reasonable on the evidence. The appeal outcome is final
internally.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. DIGNITY AT WORK - BULLYING AND HARASSMENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  BULLYING is offensive, intimidating, malicious or insulting behaviour, or
     an abuse of power, that undermines, humiliates or injures. It can be a
     pattern of small things and frequently is.

6.2  HARASSMENT is unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic that
     violates dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading,
     humiliating or offensive environment. It does not have to be directed at
     you for you to be affected by it.

6.3  It is judged by its effect, not by its intention. "I was only joking" is
     not a defence and never has been.

6.4  Examples we have actually dealt with: persistent undermining of a
     colleague in meetings; comments about an employee's accent; exclusion of
     a part-time colleague from decisions taken on days she did not work;
     repeated "banter" about a colleague's age; a manager setting
     unachievable deadlines for one person and not others.

6.5  THIRD PARTIES. Harassment by a customer, supplier or visitor is covered.
     If a customer behaves this way towards you, tell your manager. We will
     act, including by declining to send you to that site or by escalating to
     the customer's own management. We have done both. Your relationship with
     a customer never requires you to absorb it.

     Employers in the UK have a positive duty to take reasonable steps to
     prevent sexual harassment of their workers, including by third parties.
     We take that seriously and our field-based staff are the most exposed to
     it.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. IF YOU HAVE BEEN ACCUSED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  You will be told what is alleged, in enough detail to respond.
7.2  You have the right to be accompanied and to put your account.
7.3  You are presumed innocent until the process concludes.
7.4  Confidentiality applies to you too, and discussing the case with
     colleagues may itself become a disciplinary matter.
7.5  Support is available to you as well - the EAP is for everyone involved.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. NO VICTIMISATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  Nobody may be treated badly for raising a grievance, supporting someone
     else's, or acting as a witness or companion. Victimisation is gross
     misconduct.

8.2  This includes after the process ends and includes a grievance that was
     not upheld.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
9. ADJUSTMENTS, ACCESSIBILITY AND ANYTHING ELSE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
9.1  If something about your work is harder than it needs to be because of a
     disability, a health condition, neurodivergence, caring responsibilities
     or anything else, you do not need to raise a grievance to get it changed.
     Speak to your manager or the People team.

9.2  You do not need a diagnosis and you do not need to use the word
     disability. We have made adjustments for people with no diagnosis at all,
     because the adjustment worked.

9.3  Your manager is told what adjustment to make. They are not told why
     unless you choose to tell them.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
10. CONTRACTORS AND AGENCY WORKERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
May raise concerns about their treatment at Veridia through the People team or
the speak-up line. The dignity at work standards apply to how they are treated
and to how they behave.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
11. CONFIDENTIALITY AND RECORDS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Grievance records are held by the People team, retained for 6 years after
employment ends, and are not placed on your general personnel file unless a
formal outcome requires it. Your manager is told what they need to know to act
on the outcome, not the detail of your evidence.
