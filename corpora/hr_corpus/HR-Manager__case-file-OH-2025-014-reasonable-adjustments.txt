     Strong. Her stated reason for referring herself was that she was
     "spending an enormous amount of effort compensating and it is not

1.3  Extract from her email, quoted with her written permission for the
     purposes of this file:

       "I want to be clear I'm not struggling with the work. I'm struggling

2. INITIAL MEETING
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Date: 2025-04-08. Present: Maya Rothbury, Rachel Duffy. Held off site at
Maya's request.

2.1  Discussed what she wanted from the process. She was clear that she did
     not want her manager told the diagnosis, and she was anxious that
     disclosure would change how her technical judgement was regarded.
     Rachel confirmed that consent to share is her decision, that a manager
     can be told what to do without being told why, and that this is common.

2.2  Discussed whether ADHD would meet the definition of a disability under
     the Equality Act 2010. Rachel's position, and it is the People team's
     standard position, is that we do not require the question to be settled
     before making an adjustment. If an adjustment is reasonable and it works,
     we make it. Whether a tribunal would classify the condition is a question
     for a tribunal, and waiting for legal certainty before helping someone is
     both slow and unkind.

2.3  Consent obtained in writing for an occupational health referral, on a
     partial-disclosure basis: the report to state functional effects and
     recommended adjustments, and not to state the diagnosis to the manager.

3. OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH REPORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Provider: Optima Health. Assessed by Dr Ranjit Sanghera, 2025-04-22. Report
received 2025-04-24. Maya reviewed the report on 2025-04-25 and had no
comments.

Summary of findings:
  - Fit for work in her current role without restriction.
  - Functional effects: difficulty sustaining attention in environments with
    competing auditory stimuli; working memory load in verbal-only handovers;
    task initiation difficulty under ambiguous prioritisation; time
    estimation.
  - Strengths noted, and the report is unusual in recording them: sustained
    hyperfocus on complex debugging; high tolerance for ambiguity in technical
    problems as distinct from organisational ones.
  - Prognosis: stable condition, managed with medication (initiated
    2025-03-26, titration ongoing at the time of assessment).

Recommended adjustments:
  1. Noise-cancelling headphones; sanctioned use during focused work.
  2. Bookable quiet space on office days.
  3. Written follow-up to any verbal instruction or verbal handover.
  4. Agenda circulated in advance of any meeting where she is expected to
     contribute.
  5. Flexible start time; core hours attendance from 10:30 rather than 10:00.
  6. Support with task chunking and explicit prioritisation from the manager
     in one-to-ones.
  7. Where possible, avoid assigning more than two concurrent escalations.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. LEARNING FOR THE PEOPLE TEAM
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  This is our first ADHD-related self-referral and it went well largely
     because Maya initiated it early. Most neurodivergence cases reach us via
     a performance conversation, by which point there is damage to repair.

7.2  Worth considering: making explicit in the onboarding materials that
     adjustments can be requested proactively and do not require a problem to
     have occurred first. Raised with Naomi Okonjo-Bright, 2025-11-04.

7.3  The partial-consent model worked. Maya got what she needed and disclosed
     nothing she did not want to. Managers do not need diagnoses to manage
     well.
