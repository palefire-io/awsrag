-----------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE COMPLAINT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  Marta Oliveira raised a grievance on 2025-06-17 concerning the conduct of
     a senior biomedical scientist at a Trust she visits routinely, over a
     period of approximately five months from February 2025.

1.2  The conduct alleged:
     - Repeated comments about her appearance, described by her as "daily,
       every visit, the first thing said".
     - Persistent questions about her personal life and relationship status
       after she had said she did not want to discuss it.
     - Comments to her about her physical proximity while she worked at an
       instrument, including on one occasion standing close enough that she
       "could not step back without touching the bench".
     - Being told, when she raised it lightly on one occasion, that she
       "should take it as a compliment" and that "the last engineer didn't
       mind".
     - On 2025-06-11, a comment about her body made in the presence of two
       other Trust staff, neither of whom intervened.

1.3  Marta stated she had not reported it earlier because she believed it was
     "part of the job", because she did not want to damage the customer
     relationship, and because she was concerned she would be removed from the
     account and that it would look like a problem with her.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. INVESTIGATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  Priya Anand met Marta on 2025-06-18, the day after the grievance was
     raised. First action agreed at that meeting: Marta was removed from that
     site with immediate effect, with cover provided by another engineer, and
     was told explicitly that this was a protective measure and not a
     consequence.

2.2  Evidence considered:
     - Marta's written account and two interviews.
     - Her visit reports and diary for the period, which corroborate the
       frequency and dates of visits.
     - Contemporaneous messages between Marta and a colleague, Kasia Nowak,
       dated 2025-03-04, 2025-04-22 and 2025-06-11, in which she described
       three of the incidents at the time. Provided voluntarily by Marta with
       Kasia's agreement.
     - An interview with Kasia Nowak (2025-06-26), who confirmed the messages
       and stated Marta had raised it with her informally on several occasions
       and had said she did not want to make a formal complaint.
     - An interview with Rob Ellery (2025-06-30), who covered the site twice
       during Marta's annual leave in April and who described the same
       individual as "a bit much" but had experienced no comparable conduct
       himself.

2.3  Veridia did not interview the respondent. He is not a Veridia employee
     and we have no authority to compel or conduct an investigation into a
     third party's conduct. This is stated explicitly because it is a genuine
     limitation of any third-party harassment process and it must not be
     presented to a complainant as a full investigation of the individual.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. FINDINGS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  The grievance is UPHELD.

3.2  Marta's account is detailed, internally consistent, corroborated
     contemporaneously by messages sent at the time to a colleague, and
     consistent with her visit records. She has no motive to fabricate and
     raised it reluctantly rather than readily.

3.3  The conduct described amounts to harassment related to sex within the
     meaning of section 26 of the Equality Act 2010. It was unwanted, it was
     related to a protected characteristic, and it had the effect of violating
     her dignity and creating an intimidating and humiliating environment for
     her. The respondent's stated view that she should take it as a compliment
     is not relevant to that test; the test is effect, not intention.

3.4  A SECONDARY FINDING AGAINST VERIDIA. Marta's belief that this was "part
     of the job" did not come from nowhere. Nothing in her induction, her
     field service training or her ongoing management told her otherwise. The
     dignity at work policy in force at the time (version 3) did not mention
     third parties at all.

     Priya Anand's note: "She thought absorbing this was what a good engineer
     does. I have to accept that is what we taught her, by never saying
     anything different."

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. ACTIONS TAKEN
-----------------------------------------------------------------
FOR MARTA
  4.1  Removed from the site permanently, at her choice, with the account
       reallocated. Confirmed in writing that this carries no implication for
       her performance, her territory or her progression.
  4.2  Offered EAP support and, separately, six sessions of specialist
       counselling funded outside the EAP allowance. She took the EAP.
  4.3  Asked what outcome she wanted. Her answer, recorded: "I want someone to
       tell him. And I want the next person who goes in there not to have to
       work it out on their own."
  4.4  Kept informed at every stage, including of the escalation to the Trust
       and of its outcome.

WITH THE CUSTOMER
  4.5  James Okafor and Priya Anand met the Trust's Head of Pathology on
       2025-07-15. The meeting was requested at short notice and the reason
       was stated plainly.
  4.6  Veridia set out the conduct, named the individual, and stated that we
       would not send any engineer to that site while the matter was
       unaddressed. We did not ask for a particular outcome and did not
       attempt to direct the Trust's own process.
  4.7  The Trust conducted its own investigation under its own procedures. It
       concluded on 2025-09-12. The Trust confirmed to Veridia that the matter
       had been dealt with under its disciplinary procedure and that the
       individual would have no further involvement with Veridia visits. It
       did not disclose the sanction and Veridia did not ask - that is the
       Trust's employee's personal data and none of our business.
  4.8  The Trust's Head of Pathology wrote to Marta directly, with her
       permission and via Veridia, to apologise. Marta described that as "the
       thing that actually mattered".

  4.9  COMMERCIAL NOTE, recorded because it was raised. Michael Ashworth asked
       during the case whether the escalation risked the account, which is at
       renewal in 2027. Naomi Okonjo-Bright's answer, recorded verbatim at her
       request: "If we would not raise this because of a renewal, we should
       not be selling to the NHS. The answer is no. Raise it."
       No commercial consequence followed. The account renewed discussions
       normally.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. SYSTEMIC ACTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  5.1  Dignity at work policy revised to version 4, adding an explicit
       third-party harassment section (section 6.5) stating that harassment by
       a customer is covered, that engineers may leave a site, and that the
       relationship with a customer never requires an employee to absorb it.
       Effective 2025-04-01 - the revision was already in progress and this
       case sharpened it.

  5.2  Field service briefing delivered by Priya Anand on 2025-10-24,
       alongside the stress risk assessment outcome, covering the same point.
       Marta reviewed the briefing content before it was delivered and
       approved the anonymised reference to her case.

  5.3  Lone working and site visit guidance updated: engineers instructed that
       they may leave a site at any time without prior authorisation if they
       feel unsafe or are subjected to unacceptable conduct, and must report
       afterwards rather than seek permission beforehand.

  5.4  Added to field service induction and to the customer-facing induction
       for all roles.

  5.5  Reporting route created that does not require the engineer to tell
       their account manager, so that the commercial relationship is never the
       gatekeeper.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. REFLECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  Five months elapsed between the first incident and the complaint. In that
     time Marta told a colleague on at least three occasions and told no
     manager. The colleague did not escalate because Marta asked her not to,
     which was the wrong call but an entirely human one.

6.2  We should consider whether a colleague who is told about conduct of this
     kind should be expected to escalate it even against the wishes of the
     person affected. Sunita Raval's view is that a mandatory escalation duty
     would reduce disclosure to colleagues without increasing disclosure to
     managers. Rachel Duffy's view is that colleagues should at minimum be
     equipped to say "I am not going to report this for you, and here is who
     you can tell, and I will come with you".

     The second approach has been adopted for the training. No mandatory
     reporting duty has been introduced.

6.3  Marta agreed to the anonymised use of this case in training. She was
     asked twice, at an interval, and confirmed both times.
