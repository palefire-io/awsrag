Your First 90 Days
=================================================================
Document:      HR-GDE-005
Version:       3
Effective:     2025-09-01
Owner:         Rachel Duffy, HR Business Partner
Audience:      New starters and their managers

This is a guide, not a policy. Nothing in it is a rule. It exists because
almost every new starter asks the same questions in the same order, and it is
better to answer them once in writing.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 1 - ORIENTATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: know where things are and who people are. Nothing more.

You should by the end of the week:
  [ ] Have working accounts and a laptop that does what you need
  [ ] Have met your team and your buddy
  [ ] Have written-down goals for your first month
  [ ] Understand at a basic level what Veridia sells and to whom
  [ ] Know what to do if you think a product is unsafe
  [ ] Know how to book leave and claim expenses

You should NOT by the end of the week:
  - Understand the product properly. Nobody does after a week.
  - Have delivered anything.
  - Feel useful. This is the single most common thing new starters say at day
    30 - "I felt useless for the first fortnight". Everybody does. It passes.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WEEKS 2 TO 4 - FIRST CONTRIBUTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: finish something small, end to end.

Your manager should have given you a piece of work that is real, small enough
to finish, and low enough stakes that getting it wrong costs nothing. Fixing a
documentation error, running a defined analysis, taking a category of tickets.

Things worth doing in this period:
  - Meet ten people outside your team. Ask them what they do and what makes
    their job harder. This is the single highest-return activity available to
    a new starter and almost nobody does enough of it.
  - Read the last six months of your team's output, whatever form it takes.
  - Write down every time something confuses you. You will lose the ability to
    see it within about eight weeks, and that list is genuinely valuable to us.
    Several policy changes have come out of new starter confusion lists.
  - Ask your buddy the questions you think are too stupid to ask your manager.

DAY 30: structured review with your manager against the month one goals, plus
a short survey from the People team. The survey is not a formality. The free
text is read by Rachel Duffy and Naomi Okonjo-Bright, and it has changed the
onboarding policy twice - once about managers being absent in a starter's
first week, and once about laptops arriving late.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WEEKS 5 TO 8 - BUILDING UP
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: work at something closer to a normal load, with support.

By now:
  - You should be taking work without it being handed to you individually.
  - You should be able to explain what your team does to someone outside it.
  - Field service engineers: supervised customer visits begin around week 8.
  - Support engineers: independent ticket ownership from around week 5.

DAY 60: manager review. This is the honest one. If your manager has any
concern about your probation, you hear it at day 60, clearly, with what needs
to change and by when. Nobody at Veridia should ever be surprised at day 90.
If you get to day 60 and your manager says "all fine" and then raises a
problem at day 90, that is a failure by the manager and you should tell the
People team.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WEEKS 9 TO 13 - INDEPENDENCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: be doing the job.

DAY 90: probation decision. See User__policy-probation-period-v3.txt. Three
possible outcomes: confirmed, extended, or not confirmed. The overwhelming
majority are confirmed.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS NEW STARTERS ACTUALLY ASK
-----------------------------------------------------------------
"When can I book holiday?"
  Immediately. You accrue from day one and you can book from day one, subject
  to your manager's approval like everyone else. You do not have to wait for
  probation. See User__policy-annual-leave-v5.txt.

"Am I allowed to disagree with my manager?"
  Yes, and in the first three months your outside perspective is the most
  valuable thing you have. Say it once, clearly, and then commit to whatever
  is decided.

"How much do people work?"
  Contracted hours are 37.5 a week in the UK and 40 in Switzerland. Most
  people work close to that. There are periods - a product launch, a
  regulatory submission, a field safety notice - when some teams work more,
  and those periods should be visible and time-limited. If your team is
  permanently in one, that is a problem to raise, not a culture to admire.

"Everyone seems to know about a reagent recall in 2025. What is that?"
  FSN-2025-003. A batch of lyse reagent developed a fault that produced
  spurious white cell flags on the VeriScan 400. We recalled 21 lots. It is
  discussed openly in induction because of how it was found and what we
  changed afterwards, and because it is the clearest example we have of why
  the quality system exists.

"Who do I talk to if something is wrong and it is not about my work?"
  Your manager, the People team, or the speak-up route in
  User__policy-whistleblowing-speak-up-v3.txt, which can be anonymous.

"When will I stop feeling behind?"
  Most people say around month four. Technical roles in the regulated part of
  the business often say month six. This is normal and is not a reflection on
  you.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
FOR MANAGERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The three things that make the difference, in order:
  1. Written goals for month one, set before day one.
  2. An honest day 60 conversation.
  3. Being present. A new starter whose manager is unavailable for their first
     fortnight takes roughly twice as long to become productive, based on our
     own day 30 survey data.
