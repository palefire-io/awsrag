WHAT CHANGED IN VERSION 5
  - L4 split into an individual contributor track and a management track on
    the same band (section 4). Previously the only route past L3 was to manage
    people, which we were repeatedly told was wrong and which we agree was
    wrong.
  - Promotion windows moved from one a year to two (section 6).
  - Levelling guides published in full rather than summarised. See
    User__levelling-guide-engineering-v5.txt and
    User__levelling-guide-commercial-and-support-v3.txt.
  - Band ranges are no longer published in this document. See section 8 for
    why, and what you can ask for instead.

1. WHY WE HAVE A FRAMEWORK
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Veridia grew from 40 people to about 320 in nine years. For most of that time
levels were decided case by case, which worked when everybody could name
everybody and stopped working somewhere around 150 people.

The framework exists to answer three questions consistently:
  - What is expected of someone at this level?
  - What would I need to demonstrate to move to the next one?
  - Is my pay in the right place for the work I actually do?

It is not a performance rating, it is not a queue, and time served in a level
is not by itself a reason to move up.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE LEVELS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  L1  Associate           Learning the craft. Well-defined work, close support.
  L2  Engineer            Owns defined pieces of work end to end.
  L3  Senior              Owns an area. Others rely on your judgement in it.
  L4  Staff / Principal   Owns a system or a department. Your decisions shape
      or Head of          how other people work.
  L5  Director            Owns a function, its budget and its plan.
  L6  Executive           Executive Committee.

The full title list by job family is in
User__lookup-job-levels-and-titles.csv.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT MOVES YOU UP A LEVEL
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Three things, and all three have to be true.

  SCOPE       The size and ambiguity of what you are trusted with. Not how
              hard you work. A person doing L3 work at L2 pace is doing L3
              work.

  IMPACT      What changed because you did it. We are a small company and
              almost everyone can point at something. The question is whether
              the something is the size of the level.

  BEHAVIOUR   How you got there. The behaviours in the code of conduct are
              not a separate track; sustained impact achieved by making
              colleagues' lives worse does not qualify anyone for anything.

A promotion recognises that you have been operating at the next level. It is
not a bet that you will. If you are being told "do the job for six months and
we will review it", that is a conversation about scope, not about promotion,
and your manager should be explicit about which one you are having.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE TWO TRACKS AT L4 AND ABOVE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
From L4 there are two tracks:

  Individual contributor    Staff Engineer, Principal Scientist. Depth. You
                            are trusted with the hardest technical problems
                            and your judgement carries beyond your team.

  Management                Head of, Director. Breadth. You are accountable
                            for a group of people, their work and their
                            development.

They sit on the same band. Moving between them is lateral. If you move from
Head of X to Staff Engineer, your pay does not change and it is not a
demotion, and anyone who describes it as one is wrong.

We added this in version 5 because in the 2024 engagement survey "the only way
up is to manage people" was the single most common free-text comment, appearing
31 times. It was a fair criticism.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. LEVELS ARE NOT TITLES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Your level is internal. Your title is what appears on your email signature and
your business card, and in customer-facing roles it sometimes needs to say
something the customer will understand.

Two consequences:
  - Two people with the same title can be at different levels.
  - You may negotiate a title change with your manager without any change of
    level or pay. Titles are cheap and we would rather you had one that helps
    you do your job.

What we will not do is inflate a title to substitute for a promotion.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. HOW AND WHEN PROMOTIONS HAPPEN
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Two windows a year, aligned to the performance cycle:

  Spring window   Nominations close 15 March. Decisions communicated by
                  30 April. Effective 1 May.
  Autumn window   Nominations close 15 September. Decisions communicated by
                  31 October. Effective 1 November.

Process:
  1. You or your manager raises it. Either is fine; you do not need to wait to
     be nominated and you do not need your manager's permission to be
     considered.
  2. Your manager writes a case against the levelling guide, with evidence.
  3. Cases go to a calibration panel for the job family, chaired by the
     relevant Director with the People team present. The panel exists so that
     the bar is the same across teams, not to ration outcomes.
  4. The decision, and the reasoning, comes back to you from your manager in
     person. If the answer is no, you are entitled to know specifically what
     was missing.

There is no quota. If eleven people meet the bar, eleven people get promoted.
Budget constrains the pay review (see section 8), not the level.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT HAPPENS TO PAY ON PROMOTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A promotion moves you to the new band. Where in the new band you land depends
on where you were, and it is normal to enter a band below its midpoint.

If your current pay is already inside the new band, you will still receive an
increase; a promotion with no pay movement is not something we do.

Promotion increases are separate from the annual pay review and are not
deducted from it.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. A NOTE ON PAY RANGES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Versions 1 to 4 of this document published the numeric range for each band.
Version 5 does not, and we owe you an explanation rather than a silent
removal.

We publish band ranges to managers, not to the whole company, because with 320
people across two countries the ranges are wide enough that individuals were
reading them as a statement about their own pay and drawing wrong conclusions -
most often that being below a midpoint meant something was wrong, when in a
band that spans 40 per cent it usually means length of service in the level.

That is a reason to explain better, not to hide, so:

  - You are entitled to know your own band, where you sit in it, and why. Ask
    your manager. If you do not get a straight answer, ask the People team and
    you will get one.
  - You are entitled to know what the band ranges are for your own level. Ask
    and we will tell you.
  - We publish the gender pay gap annually and will continue to.

This is a compromise and we know some of you will think it is the wrong one.
It is reviewed every year and the People team would rather hear the argument
than not.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
9. IF YOU DISAGREE WITH YOUR LEVEL
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Talk to your manager first. If that does not resolve it, ask for a review by
the People team - Rachel Duffy for Cambridge and remote UK, Lena Hofstetter
for Basel. A review looks at the evidence against the levelling guide with
fresh eyes. It can go up, and it can also confirm the original.

If you believe your level or pay has been affected by discrimination, that is
a grievance, not a levelling question, and it should go through
User__policy-grievance-and-dignity-at-work-v4.txt.
