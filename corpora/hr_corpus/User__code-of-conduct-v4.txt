1. WHY THIS ONE MATTERS MORE THAN MOST
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Most companies' codes of conduct are written by lawyers to protect the
company. This one partly is, and Sunita wrote it. But there is a reason it is
taken more seriously here than the genre usually deserves.

We make medical devices. A result from a VeriScan analyser can be the thing
that tells a clinician a patient's white cell count is rising. If we cut a
corner, the consequence is not an unhappy customer, it is a wrong number in
front of a doctor.

That single fact sits behind most of what follows.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. PRODUCT SAFETY AND QUALITY - THE FIRST OBLIGATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  If you believe a Veridia product is unsafe, is not performing as it
     should, or that a quality or regulatory obligation is not being met, you
     must report it. This is not discretionary and it is not something you can
     delegate to somebody more senior.

2.2  Report to: your manager, Sarah Lindgren (Regulatory Affairs), Simone
     Buehler (QA), or anonymously through the speak-up channel. Any of these
     is a valid route and you do not have to try one before another.

2.3  Veridia will not retaliate. Not in your appraisal, not in your pay
     review, not in a restructure, not socially. Retaliation is a dismissable
     offence and it applies to the most senior people in the company.

2.4  You will not be criticised for raising a concern that turns out to be
     unfounded, provided you raised it honestly. We would far rather
     investigate ten things that turn out fine than miss one that does not.

2.5  Never falsify, backdate or destroy a quality record, a training record, a
     calibration record or a complaint record. Never sign for work you did not
     do or witness. Never mark a task complete that is not. These are not
     administrative matters; they are the things auditors and regulators look
     at and they are the things that go badly wrong.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW WE TREAT EACH OTHER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Bullying, harassment, discrimination and victimisation are not tolerated.
     This includes conduct that is not intended to cause harm but does.

3.2  It applies at work, at customer sites, at conferences, at company events,
     in messaging channels, and at the pub afterwards. The last one is where
     most of the incidents we have dealt with actually occurred.

3.3  Disagreement is not disrespect. We want people to argue about technical
     decisions and to say the unwelcome thing. The line is between attacking
     an idea and attacking a person, and most people know exactly where it is.

3.4  Seniority is not a licence. The more senior you are the more your
     behaviour sets the standard, and the more damage impatience does.

3.5  If someone tells you your behaviour is affecting them, the correct first
     response is to listen, not to explain your intention.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONFIDENTIALITY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Do not discuss a named customer outside Veridia. Not on social media, not
     at a conference bar, not with a friend who works at a hospital. Our
     customers tell us about their instrument failures, their staffing
     problems and their accreditation anxieties on the understanding that it
     stays with us.

4.2  Do not share commercially sensitive information - pricing, roadmaps,
     tender responses, customer volumes.

4.3  Patient data. VeriConnect may hold patient identifiers on behalf of
     customers. You will encounter it if you work in support or field service.
     You may access it only where a specific support case requires it, and
     every access is logged and attributable to you. Curiosity is not a
     legitimate purpose.

4.4  Confidentiality obligations survive your employment.

4.5  Confidentiality never prevents you from raising a safety concern, making
     a protected disclosure, reporting discrimination, or speaking to a
     regulator. Nothing in any Veridia agreement is intended to have that
     effect, and if you are ever presented with a document that appears to,
     tell the General Counsel.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Declare anything that could reasonably look like a conflict. The test is
     how it would look written down by someone unsympathetic.

5.2  Declarable: a financial interest in a competitor, customer or supplier;
     a close personal relationship with someone at a customer or supplier; a
     second job or consultancy; a relative applying for a job here; a
     directorship elsewhere.

5.3  RELATIONSHIPS AT WORK. Consenting relationships between colleagues are
     nobody's business except where one party manages, assesses, or influences
     the pay or progression of the other. In that case it must be declared to
     the People team so that the reporting line or the assessment can be
     changed. The declaration is confidential. Nobody is in trouble for the
     relationship; people would be in trouble for concealing it while sitting
     on the other's pay review.

5.4  Declare to your manager and to Sunita Raval. The register is confidential.
     Declaring something is never held against you.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. GIFTS AND HOSPITALITY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  We sell to publicly funded healthcare systems. The rules are stricter
     than in most industries and the reputational exposure is asymmetric.

6.2  NHS and public sector customers:
       - Never offer or give cash or a cash equivalent.
       - Modest hospitality directly connected to a legitimate business
         purpose is acceptable - lunch at a customer meeting, refreshments at
         a user group.
       - Anything beyond that needs prior approval from your Director and
         Sunita Raval.
       - Never anything that could influence, or appear to influence, a
         procurement decision. If a tender is live, the answer is no.

6.3  Receiving: you may accept a gift of nominal value (a pen, chocolates).
     Anything over GBP 50 / CHF 50 must be declared and will usually be
     donated or raffled.

6.4  If in doubt, ask before, not after. Nobody has ever been criticised for
     asking.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. ANTI-BRIBERY AND CORRUPTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  We operate under the UK Bribery Act 2010, which is extraterritorial and
     applies to Veridia's conduct anywhere in the world.

7.2  Never offer, give, request or accept a bribe. Never make a facilitation
     payment, even where it is customary locally. If you are asked for one,
     refuse and report it - to Sunita Raval the same day.

7.3  This applies to anyone acting on Veridia's behalf, including
     distributors and agents. If you manage one, you are responsible for
     knowing how they behave.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. COMPETITION LAW
-----------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  Never discuss pricing, market allocation, bid strategy or customer
     allocation with a competitor. Not at a trade association, not at a
     conference, not informally.

8.2  If such a conversation starts, say clearly that you cannot take part,
     leave, and report it to Sunita Raval. Being present and silent is not a
     defence.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
9. USE OF COMPANY SYSTEMS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
9.1  Reasonable personal use is fine.

9.2  Do not install unapproved software on a device that touches customer data
     or the quality system.

9.3  Do not put company or customer information into a third-party service -
     including AI tools - that has not been approved. Approved tools are
     listed on the intranet. This is not a prohibition on AI; several teams
     use approved tools daily. It is a prohibition on pasting a customer's
     confidential incident report into an unapproved one.

9.4  Report a lost or stolen device immediately, at any hour.
     security@veridiabio.com or +44 1223 496 199.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
10. SPEAKING PUBLICLY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 You may say you work at Veridia and describe your role. You are not a
     spokesperson.

10.2 Media enquiries go to marketing. Regulatory enquiries go to Sarah
     Lindgren. Legal enquiries go to Sunita Raval. Never answer directly, even
     to say "no comment" - forward it.

10.3 Publishing, presenting or speaking about your work: encouraged, with
     prior approval from your Director. We have never refused one that did not
     contain confidential customer information.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
11. WHEN THE CODE IS BREACHED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 Handled under the disciplinary procedure, proportionately. Most breaches
     are mistakes and are handled as learning.

11.2 Some things are gross misconduct: falsifying a quality or regulatory
     record; deliberately concealing a product safety concern; retaliation
     against someone who raised one; bribery; serious harassment; deliberate
     misuse of patient data.

11.3 The code applies to everyone. Two of the four formal code investigations
     since 2022 concerned people at L4 or above.
