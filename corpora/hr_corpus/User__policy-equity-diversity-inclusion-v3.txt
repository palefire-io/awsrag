1. POSITION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  Veridia does not discriminate on the basis of age, disability, gender
     reassignment, marriage or civil partnership, pregnancy or maternity,
     race, religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation. In Switzerland the
     equivalent protections under federal and cantonal law apply.

1.2  We also do not discriminate on the basis of things that are not
     protected characteristics but frequently operate as proxies: which
     university you went to, whether you went to one at all, your accent, your
     socioeconomic background, or whether you took a conventional career path.

1.3  This policy states what we do, what our numbers are, and where we are
     falling short. A policy that only states aspirations is not worth
     reading.

3. WHAT THESE NUMBERS TELL US
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Our pay gap is not primarily a pay gap. Analysis at each level shows a
     mean difference of 1.1% within band, which is small but not zero and
     which we look at every review cycle. The 11.4% headline is driven almost
     entirely by distribution: we have more men at L4 and above and more men
     in the higher-paid technical functions.

3.2  That makes it a progression and a hiring problem, which is harder to fix
     than a pay problem but more worth fixing.

3.3  Field service at 11% women is our worst number and has barely moved in
     three years. It reflects the industry, and that is an explanation rather
     than an excuse.

3.4  Disclosure rates matter. A 68% disclosure rate on disability means our 9%
     figure is a floor, not a measurement.

4. WHAT WE DO ABOUT HIRING
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Structured interviews with the same questions and a scoring rubric agreed
    before the first candidate is seen.
  - Diverse interview panels wherever possible. Where it is not possible - and
    in field service it often is not - we say so rather than pretend.
  - Job adverts checked for gendered language. Requirements limited to what
    the job actually needs, because long "essential" lists deter candidates
    who do not tick everything, disproportionately women.
  - No degree requirement unless the role genuinely requires one. Most do not.
  - Salary range published in every advert. This is the single most effective
    anti-bias measure we have adopted; it removes the negotiation gap at the
    point it opens.
  - Adjustments offered to every candidate at every stage, proactively.
  - We do not ask candidates for their current salary. Anywhere. Ever.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WE DO ABOUT PROGRESSION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Calibration panels for promotion, with the People team present, so that
    the bar is comparable across teams.
  - The L4 IC track, so that technical depth is a route to seniority. Its
    absence disproportionately affected people who did not want to manage.
  - Promotion and pay review outcomes analysed by gender, ethnicity and
    part-time status every cycle. The analysis goes to the Executive
    Committee.
  - Family leave that does not penalise the person who takes it, and 8 weeks
    of secondary carer leave, because the distribution of care is the
    mechanism behind most of the progression gap.
  - Pay reviews apply to people on family leave on the same basis as everyone
    else.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT WE DO NOT DO
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  We do not use quotas or positive discrimination. Selection is on merit.

6.2  We do not set diversity targets for individual managers' bonuses. We
     think this produces gaming rather than change.

6.3  We do not run unconscious bias training as a standalone intervention. The
     evidence that it changes behaviour on its own is weak. We put the effort
     into process design instead, where the evidence is better.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. INCLUSION IN PRACTICE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Employee networks: currently Women in Veridia, Veridia Pride, and a
    Carers network. Any five employees may start one and get a budget and
    time.
  - Meetings scheduled within core hours so that people with caring
    responsibilities are not systematically excluded.
  - Accessibility of our own sites reviewed annually.
  - Religious observance accommodated - prayer space at Cambridge and Basel,
    flexibility around fasting periods and religious holidays.
  - Trans and non-binary colleagues: we support transition at work, will
    change records and email addresses promptly on request, and will do so
    without requiring any medical evidence. A colleague's gender history is
    confidential and disclosing it without consent may be unlawful and is a
    disciplinary matter.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHERE WE HAVE FALLEN SHORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Included deliberately.

  - Field service diversity has not moved. We have tried adjusting adverts and
    it has not worked. We do not currently have a better idea and are looking
    for one.
  - The L4 IC track took two years longer than it should have, and the 2024
    engagement survey told us so 31 times before we acted.
  - Our disability disclosure rate of 68% suggests people do not feel safe
    disclosing. That is a signal about us, not about them.
  - Our ethnicity representation at L4 and above is too small a population to
    report responsibly, which is itself the finding.
