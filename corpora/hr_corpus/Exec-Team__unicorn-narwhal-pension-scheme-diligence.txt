4. THE CHANGE OF CONTROL - WHAT WILL ACTUALLY HAPPEN
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  A share purchase does not trigger a section 75 debt, because the
     participating employers do not change. That is the good news and it is
     the extent of the good news.

4.2  The trustees will assess whether the transaction weakens the employer
     covenant. On the facts they will conclude that it does, or at least that
     it changes it in ways requiring mitigation, because:

     (a) Veridia is SMALLER than Narwhal by revenue (GBP 42.1M against
         GBP 58.2M) and by headcount. The covenant would move from a business
         with GBP 58M of revenue to a group whose parent is smaller than the
         scheme's current sponsor.

     (b) The transaction introduces GBP 18M of new acquisition debt which
         ranks AHEAD of the scheme. This is the point trustees care about most
         and it is the point on which the 2025 process is understood to have
         failed.

     (c) Veridia is Series D venture-backed and not profitable at the level
         Narwhal is. Our EBITDA is lower in absolute terms. A trustee looking
         at our accounts will see a growth company, and growth companies are
         not what a pension trustee wants standing behind a GBP 19.4M buyout
         deficit.

     (d) The scheme would become a materially larger liability relative to its
         sponsor. Currently the buyout deficit is 33% of sponsor revenue.
         Post-transaction it would be 19% of combined revenue but would sit
         behind acquisition debt in a leveraged structure.

4.3  The trustees will appoint a covenant adviser. They have not yet and they
     will the moment they are told. Expect a covenant assessment taking six to
     ten weeks and costing the scheme (and therefore ultimately the employer)
     GBP 60,000 to GBP 90,000.

4.4  LIKELY TRUSTEE ASKS, in Pilkington Rowe's assessment and in descending
     order of likelihood:

     (i)   A PARENT COMPANY GUARANTEE from Veridia BioSystems Ltd, capped at
           some proportion of the section 75 debt. Near certain. Expect them
           to open at 100% of buyout (GBP 19.4M) and to settle between 50%
           and 75%.

     (ii)  AN ACCELERATED RECOVERY PLAN. From GBP 1.15M to somewhere between
           GBP 1.8M and GBP 2.4M per annum, and a shortened end date.
           Very likely. Cost in NPV terms GBP 3.5M to GBP 5.5M.

     (iii) A LUMP SUM CONTRIBUTION AT COMPLETION. GBP 3M to GBP 6M. Likely,
           and often the cleanest thing to concede because it is finite.

     (iv)  NEGATIVE PLEDGES AND INFORMATION RIGHTS - restrictions on further
           leverage, on dividends, on disposals; quarterly management accounts
           to the trustees. Certain, and cheap in cash terms but constraining.

     (v)   SECURITY OVER ASSETS. Possible. Would conflict directly with the
           acquisition lenders' security package and is the ask most likely to
           break the financing structure. Resist.

4.5  CENTRAL CASE for modelling: a lump sum of GBP 4M at completion, an
     accelerated recovery plan adding GBP 0.75M per annum to 2031
     (NPV approximately GBP 3.2M), and a parent company guarantee at 60% of
     buyout. Total economic cost GBP 5.5M to GBP 7.2M.

4.6  The GBP 5.5M figure carried in the HR due diligence report and in the
     board paper is the LOW END of this range. The Board should be presented
     with GBP 7.2M as the planning assumption at Gate 2.

5. THE PENSIONS REGULATOR
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  TPR's moral hazard powers - contribution notices and financial support
     directions - are engaged by an act that is materially detrimental to the
     likelihood of accrued benefits being received. A leveraged acquisition by
     a smaller sponsor is squarely the kind of transaction TPR has in mind.

5.2  Since the Pension Schemes Act 2021 there are also criminal offences of
     avoidance of employer debt and of conduct risking accrued scheme
     benefits, each carrying up to seven years' imprisonment and an unlimited
     fine, and a civil penalty of up to GBP 1M. These apply to individuals as
     well as to companies.

     Pilkington Rowe's view is that a properly mitigated transaction with an
     engaged trustee board carries no realistic criminal exposure. We record
     the point because directors should be aware that the regime exists and
     that the appropriate response to it is early and full engagement with the
     trustees rather than a legal argument about whether the threshold is met.

5.3  CLEARANCE. Voluntary. Provides statutory protection from the moral hazard
     powers in respect of the cleared transaction.
       Recommendation: SEEK CLEARANCE.
       Cost: GBP 80,000 to GBP 120,000 in adviser fees.
       Time: 3 to 4 months from application, and TPR will not engage until the
       trustees are supportive.
       Consequence: it puts the trustees on the critical path to completion.
       They know that, and it strengthens their negotiating position.

5.4  The alternative - completing without clearance - is lawful and is what
     most mid-market transactions do. Pilkington Rowe recommend clearance here
     specifically because the sponsor is getting smaller, which is the fact
     pattern TPR looks at hardest.

6. THE 2025 PROCESS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  Ardenmoor ran a sale process in 2025 which reached exclusivity and did not
     complete. Ardenmoor's description to us is that the buyer withdrew "on
     pension".

6.2  We have not seen the 2025 buyer's diligence and will not. What we can
     observe from the data room:
       - The trustees appointed no covenant adviser in 2025, suggesting the
         process did not reach the stage of formal covenant assessment.
       - Trustee minutes from June 2025 (redacted, in the data room) record
         the chair noting "the board's expectation that any change of control
         would be accompanied by mitigation commensurate with the change in
         covenant strength".
       - There is no record of any mitigation offer having been made.

6.3  The reasonable inference is that the 2025 buyer formed a view of the
     likely mitigation and decided it exceeded what the asset was worth to
     them. We should assume the trustees' expectations have hardened.

6.4  Cordelia Marchbanks-Hale's assessment: "You are the second buyer at this
     table in eighteen months. The trustees know that. It is not a strong
     position and you should not open by testing how little they will accept."

7. RECOMMENDATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  P1  Model GBP 7.2M of total pension cost as the PLANNING ASSUMPTION, not
      GBP 5.5M. Present the range to the Board at Gate 2 with GBP 7.2M as
      central.

  P2  Approach the trustees EARLY - before signing, not after. A trustee board
      that learns of a change of control from a press release becomes an
      adversary. One that is engaged from the outset frequently becomes
      constructive. The independent chair is experienced and reasonable and
      should be approached first, privately.

  P3  Seek clearance from TPR. Accept the timetable consequence.

  P4  Get the 2025 valuation AGREED before signing if at all possible, or
      alternatively obtain a specific price adjustment mechanism tied to the
      agreed technical provisions figure. Signing into an unagreed valuation
      transfers an open-ended risk to the buyer.

  P5  Resist security over assets. Concede on the guarantee, the lump sum and
      the negative pledges before conceding security, because security will
      break the debt financing.

  P6  Consider whether the debt quantum can be reduced. Every pound of
      acquisition debt makes the covenant conversation harder, and there is a
      point at which reducing leverage costs less than the mitigation it
      avoids. Gary Pemberton to model.

  P7  THE BOARD SHOULD DECIDE NOW what its walk-away number is on pension
      mitigation, and record it, before entering trustee negotiations. Deciding
      it in the room is how buyers end up paying more than the asset is worth.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. CONCLUSION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The scheme is not in bad shape by the standards of closed DB schemes of its
vintage. The trustees are competent and the investment strategy is sensible.

The difficulty is not the scheme. It is that Veridia is a smaller, more
leveraged, less profitable sponsor than the one the trustees currently have,
and no amount of presentation changes that. The mitigation is the price of
that fact, and it is real money that comes off what Veridia can pay for the
business.

Pilkington Rowe's view is that the transaction is achievable. It is achievable
at a total pension cost of around GBP 7M and with a parent company guarantee
that will sit on Veridia's balance sheet for a decade. Whether that is worth
paying is a commercial judgement and not ours to make.
