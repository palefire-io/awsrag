1. THE BUDGET
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  Total budget: 3.4% of the UK salary bill and 2.1% of the Swiss salary
     bill.

1.2  This is lower than 2025 (4.1% UK) and you will be asked why. The honest
     answer, which you may give: revenue grew more slowly than planned in
     FY2025, the FSN-2025-003 recall carried a direct cost of approximately
     GBP 1.4M in replacement stock, credit notes and field service time, and
     the Board approved a smaller reward envelope as a result.

1.3  You may say all of that. Do not say "there is no money" - it is not true
     and people can see the funding announcements.

1.4  The budget is NOT distributed evenly. It is allocated by function based
     on market movement and on where we have retention risk. Field service and
     firmware received above-average allocations; corporate functions received
     below-average.

1.5  Separately funded and NOT drawn from your budget:
       - Promotion increases (spring and autumn windows)
       - Statutory minimum uplifts (National Living Wage, Swiss minimums)
       - Equal pay corrections identified in the gap analysis (section 6)
       - Off-cycle retention adjustments approved by the CPO

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW TO ALLOCATE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Three inputs, in this order of weight.

2.1  POSITION IN BAND. The single largest driver. Someone at 0.85 compa-ratio
     performing well should receive more than someone at 1.15 performing
     equally well. This is how bands work.

       Compa-ratio       Guide range for a Strong performer
       ---------------   ----------------------------------
       Below 0.85        4.5% to 7.0%
       0.85 to 0.95      3.5% to 5.0%
       0.95 to 1.05      2.5% to 3.5%
       1.05 to 1.15      1.0% to 2.5%
       Above 1.15        0% to 1.0%

2.2  PERFORMANCE OUTCOME.
       Exceptional         multiply the guide range by 1.5, capped at band max
       Strong              guide range as above
       Developing          lower half of the guide range
       Below Expectations  normally 0%. Must be discussed with the People team
                           before you communicate it.

2.3  MARKET AND RETENTION. Where a specific role has moved in the market, or
     where you have a genuine retention concern, flag it to Denise Kaur with
     reasoning. Do not solve it silently by over-allocating from your budget.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. THINGS YOU MUST NOT DO
-----------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Do not spread the budget evenly. A flat 3.4% to everyone is an
     abdication, it rewards nobody, and it is visible in the data.

3.2  Do not allocate on the basis of who asked. Every year the correlation
     between "asked for a rise" and "received above-average" is checked, and
     every year it correlates with gender. In 2025 it correlated at r = 0.31.

3.3  Do not reduce an allocation because someone is on family leave, has been
     on long-term sick leave, or works part-time. People on family leave
     receive the review on the same basis as everyone else. This is a legal
     requirement as well as a policy.

3.4  Do not use pay to resolve a performance conversation you have avoided
     having.

3.5  Do not promise anything before calibration. Managers who pre-announce
     outcomes create problems that land on the People team.

3.6  Do not describe an outcome as "what the system gave me". You made the
     recommendation.

5. CALIBRATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Chaired by the function Director with the People team present.

5.2  Bring your reasoning, not just your numbers. You will be asked to justify
     any allocation above 6% or at 0%.

5.3  The People team runs an equality analysis after calibration and before
     sign-off, checking distribution by gender, ethnicity, disability
     disclosure, part-time status, and family leave taken during the year.
     Anomalies are returned to the panel with a question, not with an
     instruction.

5.4  In the 2025 cycle, three allocations were revised following that analysis
     and one function was asked to redo its distribution entirely.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. EQUAL PAY CORRECTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  The 2025 gender pay gap analysis identified a within-band mean difference
     of 1.1% across the company, and larger differences in two specific
     populations: Regulatory Affairs (women 3.2% below men at the same level)
     and Support Engineering (women 2.4% below).

6.2  Nine individual corrections have been identified and are funded centrally.
     Affected managers have been contacted individually by Denise Kaur.

6.3  If you are one of them: the correction is applied IN ADDITION to the
     normal review allocation, and it does not come from your budget. You will
     be given specific wording. Do not improvise an explanation.

6.4  The correct framing to the employee is that a pay review analysis
     identified their salary as below where it should be for their level and
     performance, and it has been corrected. Do not say "we found a gender pay
     gap and you were on the wrong side of it" - it is true in aggregate but
     it is not an accurate statement about that individual's history and it
     invites a question you cannot answer.

6.5  A regulatory affairs premium has been added to B3 and B4 for 2026 in
     response to the same analysis and to two resignations citing pay.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. COMMUNICATING THE OUTCOME
-----------------------------------------------------------------
7.1  In person, or by video for remote colleagues. Never by email and never by
     leaving a number in a system for someone to find.

7.2  Give the number, then the reasoning, then take questions. Do not lead
     with fifteen minutes of context.

7.3  If the outcome is 0% or near it, say so directly at the start and explain
     why. Almost always it is band position, and almost always the person does
     not know where they sit in their band. Tell them. They are entitled to
     know.

7.4  Expect: "Is that it?" Be ready for it. The honest answer for someone high
     in band is that their pay is already ahead of the market for their level
     and that further movement requires a change of level.

7.5  Do not compare to anyone else, even anonymously.

7.6  If someone reacts badly, do not defend. Listen, note it, and offer a
     follow-up conversation with the People team. About four people a year
     take that up.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. IF SOMEONE CHALLENGES THEIR OUTCOME
-----------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  They may ask for a People team review. Denise Kaur handles these.

8.2  A review can change an outcome and has done so twice in three years.

8.3  If someone alleges the outcome was discriminatory, that is a grievance
     and must be routed to Rachel Duffy immediately. Do not attempt to resolve
     it yourself.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
9. WHAT TO DO ABOUT A COMPETING OFFER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
9.1  Tell Denise Kaur before you say anything to the employee.

9.2  Veridia does not routinely counter-offer, and a promotion will never be
     created to retain someone.

9.3  Where a person's pay is genuinely below where it should be, we correct it
     regardless of whether they have an offer - and if you only discovered
     that because they got an offer, that is a signal about your management,
     not about them.

9.4  Do not tell anyone that a counter-offer is being considered until it is
     approved.
