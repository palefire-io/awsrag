1. PRINCIPLES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  Everybody hears it on the same day. No cascade over a week. No group
     learning from another group.

1.2  Employees hear it before customers, before the press, and before
     LinkedIn. This requires the announcement to be tightly sequenced within a
     single morning.

1.3  Say what we do not know. The instinct in acquisition communications is to
     project certainty. It fails, because employees know that a company that
     has just bought another company has not finished deciding things. Saying
     "we have not decided that yet and here is when we will" is more credible
     and, unlike false certainty, does not have to be walked back.

1.4  Never say "there are no plans for redundancies" unless it is true and
     will remain true. It is the sentence most likely to be quoted back.

1.5  Delivered by people, in person, at each site. Not by email and not by a
     recorded video. The CEO and CPO travel.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHY WE ARE DOING MORE THAN THE LAW REQUIRES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  On a share purchase there is no UK obligation to inform or consult
     employees. We could announce at completion with no notice.

2.2  We are not going to, for three reasons.

     (a) There is already speculation. Four of thirteen H2 2025 exit
         interviews referred unprompted to rumours the company was "doing
         something". The People team FAQ has carried a question about
         acquisition rumours since June 2026, answered honestly with "we
         cannot confirm or deny". Silence is not producing an absence of
         belief.

     (b) Everything else Veridia has written down promises directness. The
         speak-up policy, the pay review guidance, the FAQ. An announcement
         that contradicted that tone would cost us more than it saved.

     (c) The Polish site must be told anyway. Solidarnosc must be engaged
         under Article 26-1 of the Trade Unions Act and under the collective
         agreement. A UK population learning of the transaction after the
         Polish population would be a fiasco.

4. WHAT WE SAY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  THE CORE MESSAGE - to both populations, identical:

  "Veridia has agreed to acquire Narwhal Industrial. Together we will be a
   company of about 730 people making both the analysers and the automation
   that laboratories use.

   We bought Narwhal because they are better at mechanical engineering than we
   are. That is not a diplomatic way of saying something else - it is the
   actual reason. Our own automation product had a design problem that cost us
   eighteen months and a lot of customer goodwill, and Narwhal's engineers
   would not have made that mistake.

   Here is what we know. Here is what we do not know yet. Here is when we will
   know it."

4.2  WHAT IS NOT CHANGING - say this early and be specific:
   - Nobody's employer changes. Contracts, terms, continuous service, notice
     periods, all unchanged. This is a share purchase.
   - Nobody's pay changes downward. Ever, as a result of this.
   - Both sites continue. Warrington and Wroclaw are not being consolidated.
   - Veridia's terms are not being reduced to fund this.
   - Products, customers and commitments continue.

4.3  WHAT WE INTEND TO DO - only where we mean it:
   - Narwhal employees will move onto Veridia's pension contribution and
     annual leave at the earliest practical date. This is an improvement and
     it is a commitment.
   - The field engineers currently engaged as contractors will be offered
     employment.
   - There will be a company-wide engagement survey within 90 days, and the
     results will be published to everyone.

4.4  WHAT WE DO NOT YET KNOW - say it plainly:
   - Whether any roles will change or be reduced. WE WILL NOT SAY THERE WILL
     BE NO REDUNDANCIES. What we will say is: no decision has been taken, we
     will not make decisions about people's jobs behind closed doors, and if
     any role is affected the person will hear it directly and first, with a
     proper process.
   - What the combined organisation structure looks like. Target: 90 days.
   - Which systems will be used. Target: 6 months.
   - Where offices will be. Nothing changes for at least 12 months.

4.5  THE HARD QUESTIONS - answers agreed in advance, in the FAQ at Annex B:

  "Will there be redundancies?"
    "No decision has been taken. I am not going to promise you there will
     never be any, because I would be making that promise without knowing
     whether I could keep it, and you would remember. What I will promise is
     this: no decision about anyone's job will be made without a proper
     process, and nobody will find out from a rumour."

  "Are our terms being levelled down?"
    "No. Nobody's terms are being reduced. Where terms differ between the two
     companies, we will move towards the better of the two, and that will take
     time and money and we are committing to it."

  "Is the Wroclaw site closing?"
    "No. Wroclaw's manufacturing capability is one of the reasons for the
     acquisition. We are committing capital investment there, and Mariusz will
     hold us to it."

  "Why did you not tell us before?"
    "Because transactions like this are confidential until they are signed,
     and a leak would have damaged both companies and both sets of employees.
     I know that will feel unsatisfying. It is the honest answer."

  "How long have you known?"
    Give the real timeline. Do not obscure it. First conversation November
     2025, exclusivity March 2026, signed today.

  "How much did you pay?"
    Give the figure once it is public. Do not be coy about it with employees
     while it is in the trade press.

  "Are the executives getting bonuses out of this?"
    Answer honestly. Standish and the Narwhal management team have equity and
     will receive proceeds. Veridia executives do not receive a transaction
     bonus. Say both.

5. WHAT WE WILL NOT DO
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Use the word "synergy" in any employee communication. It has one meaning
    to an employee and it is not the finance one.
  - Say "business as usual". It is not, and saying it insults people.
  - Describe it as a merger. It is an acquisition and everyone can read.
  - Announce on a Friday.
  - Announce in December, or in the two weeks either side of the Wroclaw
    summer shutdown.
  - Let any employee learn of it from a customer, a journalist or LinkedIn.
  - Circulate the role overlap model, in any form, to anyone outside the deal
    team. If that document reaches employees, nothing else in this plan
    matters.

6. THE 90 DAYS AFTER
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Week 1-2    Daily deal team standup. Question inbox answered within 48 hours.
  Week 2-12   Weekly written update to all employees, both companies. Continue
              even in weeks where there is nothing to say - "nothing has
              changed this week" is itself information.
  Week 4      Narwhal manager listening sessions run by Rachel Duffy and
              Bernadette Astley-Quinn. No Veridia executives present.
  Week 6      Terms harmonisation announcement - pension and annual leave.
  Week 8      Contractor employment offers issued.
  Week 12     Engagement survey, both populations, results published to
              everyone including the bad ones.
  Week 12     Organisation structure announced.

6.1  THE SINGLE BIGGEST RISK IN THE 90 DAYS is a communications vacuum. In the
     absence of information people assume the worst, and the worst is
     redundancy. The weekly update exists to prevent the vacuum and must not
     be allowed to lapse when the deal team gets busy, which is exactly when
     it will be tempting.

7. FIRST DRAFT - CPO OPENING REMARKS, VERIDIA ALL-HANDS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Draft. To be revised.

  "Most of you have heard a rumour at some point in the last few months. Some
   of you asked the People team about it and were told we could not confirm or
   deny anything. That was true and I know it was frustrating.

   Here is what was happening. Since November we have been talking to a
   company called Narwhal Industrial, in Warrington, who make laboratory
   automation. This morning we signed an agreement to buy them.

   They are bigger than us. Four hundred and ten people to our three hundred
   and eighteen. From today we are a company of about seven hundred and thirty.

   I want to say three things.

   The first is that nothing about your employment changes today. Not your
   contract, not your pay, not your terms, not who you work for. This is a
   share purchase, and legally and practically your job is the same job it was
   yesterday.

   The second is that the people joining us have worse terms than you do. Less
   pension, less leave, no private medical, statutory sick pay. We are going
   to fix that, because I am not running a company where two people doing the
   same job have different lives. It will cost us money and we have decided to
   spend it. And I want to be equally clear: we are not paying for it by
   reducing anything you have.

   The third is the one you actually want the answer to, and I am not going to
   dress it up. No decision has been taken about anybody's job. I am not going
   to stand here and promise there will never be a redundancy at this company,
   because I would be promising something I cannot know, and you would remember
   that I said it.

   What I will promise is that nobody at Veridia will find out about a decision
   affecting their job from a rumour, from a colleague, or from me at an
   all-hands. You will hear it from your manager, first, individually, with a
   proper process.

   We have forty minutes for questions and I would rather use all of them."
