1. VB-0312 - Duncan Threlfall - Senior Software Engineer, L3
   Left 2025-08-29 - 4 years 2 months - to a Cambridge fintech
   Interviewed by Rachel Duffy, 2025-08-22
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Reason given: money and progression.

  "I'm not leaving unhappy. I'm leaving because they offered me twenty-two
   thousand more and an L4 title and I would have been an idiot to say no."

  "The honest version is I should have asked here first. I didn't because I
   assumed the answer. That's on me and it's also on the fact that nobody has
   ever told me what the top of my band is."

  "The IC track came in a year too late for me. If it had existed in 2023 I'd
   probably still be here."

  "Best thing about this place is that when the reagent thing happened,
   nobody lied. I've worked somewhere that would have."

Rachel's note: he is right that he should have asked. He is also right that
nothing in our process invited him to. Third departure in eighteen months
citing the pre-v5 progression structure.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
THEMES FOR THE AGGREGATE REPORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  1. WORKLOAD CONCENTRATED IN Q1 2025. Cited by 6 of 13. The recall, the
     firmware defect and the SP-1 programme landed together on the same
     people. Already addressed through the stress risk assessment; noted here
     because it continued to be cited into December, nine months later.

  2. PAY AND BAND TRANSPARENCY. Cited by 5 of 13. The recurring pattern is
     someone leaving for more money without having asked here first, because
     they did not know what the range was. This is the single most actionable
     theme and it points directly at the decision recorded in the career
     framework at section 8.

  3. PROGRESSION STRUCTURE PRE-V5. Cited by 3. Should decay now the IC track
     exists.

  4. FIELD SERVICE TRAVEL BURDEN. Cited by 2 of 2 field service leavers.

  5. MANAGERS SPOKEN OF WELL. In 11 of 13 interviews the direct manager was
     described positively and unprompted. Priya Anand and Aisha Rahman were
     each named warmly by leavers who were leaving because of workload their
     managers could not fix. That distinction matters and should be reported.

  6. SPECULATION ABOUT COMPANY DIRECTION. Four of thirteen leavers referred
     unprompted to rumours about the company "doing something", an
     acquisition, or "something big". None had any information and none
     claimed to. Two said the uncertainty had been a minor factor in their
     decision to look elsewhere.

     Reported to the CPO separately on 2026-01-08 rather than in the aggregate
     report. Rachel's note: I do not know what if anything is behind this and
     have not asked. What I can report is that people are talking, that the
     absence of information is itself producing an effect, and that in my
     experience speculation of this kind gets worse rather than better if it
     is left alone.
