2. DECOMPOSITION - WHERE THE GAP ACTUALLY COMES FROM
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This is the section that matters and it is why this document exists.

  Component                                      Contribution to mean gap
  --------------------------------------------   ------------------------
  Level distribution (more men at L4+)                    6.9 pp
  Function distribution (more men in premium
    families: software, firmware, cloud, field)           3.2 pp
  Within-level, within-function residual                  1.1 pp
  Part-time and hours effects                             0.2 pp
                                                          -------
  Total                                                  11.4 pp

2.1  Roughly 89% of the gap is structural - who is at what level and in what
     function. About 11% is a residual we cannot explain by level, function,
     tenure in level or performance outcome.

2.2  THE 1.1% RESIDUAL IS THE PART WE CAN ACT ON DIRECTLY AND IT IS NOT ZERO.
     Expressed differently: across 214 UK employees, women are paid on average
     GBP 640 a year less than men doing the same job at the same level with
     comparable performance and tenure.

3.1  REGULATORY AFFAIRS, -3.2%. Six women, four men, all L2 to L3. The gap
     traces to starting salaries: three of the four men were hired between
     2021 and 2023 when we were still asking candidates their current salary,
     and negotiated up from their stated figure. Two of the women were
     internal moves from Quality and took the band minimum. We stopped asking
     about current salary in 2023 and started publishing ranges in adverts in
     2024, which addresses the mechanism going forward but does nothing for
     the people already affected.

3.2  SUPPORT ENGINEERING, -2.4%. Eleven women, eighteen men. The gap is
     concentrated in L2, where four women sit between the 0.88 and 0.94
     compa-ratio and no man does. Reviewing individual histories, the pattern
     is that the men in the equivalent band position received off-cycle
     adjustments following competing offers and the women did not. Three of
     the four have never asked for a pay review.

3.3  The field service +0.8% is not meaningful at n=4 and should not be
     presented as a positive finding.

5. STRUCTURAL ACTIONS - THE 89%
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The residual is fixable with money. The structural gap is not, and will not
move until the distribution changes.

  5.1  L4+ female representation is 34%. Every promotion to L4 for three
       cycles has been analysed; the pass rate for women and men at L4 is
       statistically indistinguishable (68% vs 71%, n=41). THE PROBLEM IS
       NOMINATION RATE, NOT SUCCESS RATE. Women were nominated at 0.71 times
       the rate of men relative to eligible population.

  5.2  That points at managers, not at panels. Actions: nomination rates by
       manager are now reported to Directors; self-nomination has been made
       explicit in the framework and the FAQ; the People team reviews the
       eligible-but-not-nominated list before each window.

  5.3  Premium function distribution. Software, firmware, cloud and field
       service carry a market premium of 8-10% and are 76% male. This is a
       hiring pipeline problem. Field service at 11% female has not moved in
       three years despite advert changes.

  5.4  The L4 individual contributor track, introduced in framework v5, should
       help. The prior structure required people to manage to progress, and
       our data shows women at L3 declining management moves at a higher rate
       than men - most commonly citing the incompatibility of the on-call and
       travel burden with caring responsibilities.

  5.5  Secondary carer leave increased to 8 weeks for 2026. The evidence that
       secondary carer leave shifts the long-run distribution of care is
       strong, and the distribution of care is the mechanism behind most of
       the progression gap. This is the highest-leverage intervention in the
       list and it will take a decade to show up in this analysis.

7. ETHNICITY AND DISABILITY PAY GAPS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Not statutorily required and not currently published.

Ethnicity: disclosure rate 81%. Mean gap between employees reporting an ethnic
minority background and white employees is 4.9%. The population at L4+ is
small enough (n=7) that a function-level decomposition is not statistically
meaningful. Recommendation: publish the headline from 2027 once two further
years of data exist.

Disability: disclosure rate 68%, which is too low to analyse responsibly. The
low disclosure rate is itself the finding and is discussed in
User__policy-equity-diversity-inclusion-v3.txt section 8.
