1. THE WINDOWS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  SPRING     Nominations close 15 March
             Calibration late March / early April
             Communicated by 30 April
             Effective 1 May

  AUTUMN     Nominations close 15 September
             Calibration late September / early October
             Communicated by 31 October
             Effective 1 November

Two windows were introduced in version 4. One a year meant someone who reached
the bar in February waited eleven months, which was neither fair nor good for
retention.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO CAN BE NOMINATED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  Anyone. You may nominate yourself and you do not need your manager's
     permission to be considered.

2.2  There is no minimum time in level. Time served is not evidence and its
     absence is not a disqualification. Two people have been promoted within
     ten months of joining and both were correct decisions.

2.3  Being on family leave, long-term sick leave or a sabbatical does not
     remove you from consideration. Cases are assessed on the work done, and
     an absence is not counted against you.

2.4  Being on a performance support plan does normally preclude a promotion in
     that window.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT A CASE CONTAINS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Two pages, no more. Longer cases do not do better.

  1. Which level, and which levelling guide.
  2. Three to five concrete examples of work at the next level. Specific
     enough that someone outside the team can evaluate them. "Led the
     migration" is not an example; "led the HL7 interface migration for the
     Manchester network, including the decision to defer the FHIR path, which
     avoided a revalidation the customer could not have absorbed before their
     UKAS visit" is.
  3. Evidence of the "how" as well as the "what".
  4. What others say. Two or three named colleagues who have seen the work.

3.1  Your manager writes it. You should see it before it is submitted and you
     are entitled to say "that is not the strongest example of my work".

-----------------------------------------------------------------
4. CALIBRATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  All cases in a job family go to a panel chaired by the relevant Director,
     with the People team present.

4.2  The panel tests whether the evidence supports the level, and whether the
     bar is consistent with cases from other teams.

4.3  THERE IS NO QUOTA. If eleven cases meet the bar, eleven people are
     promoted. Budget constrains the pay review, not the level. In the autumn
     2025 window, 14 of 19 cases were approved.

4.4  The People team reviews outcomes for pattern by gender, ethnicity,
     part-time status and family leave.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
5. HEARING THE OUTCOME
-----------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  From your manager, in person.

5.2  If the answer is no, you are entitled to know specifically what was
     missing and what would need to be different. "Not this time" is not an
     acceptable answer and you should push back if you get one.

5.3  If the panel thought the case was close, you will be told that too.

5.4  If your manager did not put your case forward and you believe it should
     have gone, tell the People team. We will look at it. This happens once or
     twice a year and has twice resulted in a case being heard.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
6. PAY ON PROMOTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  You move to the new band. Entering below the midpoint of a new band is
     normal and is not a comment on you.

6.2  A promotion always carries a pay increase, including where your current
     pay already sits inside the new band.

6.3  Promotion increases are separate from the annual review and are not
     netted off against it.

6.4  Typical promotion increases at Veridia run between 8% and 16% depending
     on the gap between your current pay and the new band. Where a promotion
     would require a very large jump, we may stage it over two steps within
     twelve months, and if we do, we tell you the second step and the date in
     writing at the time.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT WE DO NOT DO
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Promote to retain someone with a competing offer. Pay may be addressed
    separately; a level is not.
  - Promote in place of fixing a pay problem.
  - Give a title instead of a level.
  - Hold a promotion because of headcount or budget. The level reflects the
    work; the pay is a separate conversation.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
8. LATERAL MOVES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  Moving between the IC and management tracks at L4 is lateral. Same band,
     same pay, no demotion.

8.2  Moving between functions at the same level is lateral. See
     User__policy-internal-mobility-and-secondments.txt.

8.3  Stepping back from management to an IC role is a legitimate and
     respectable choice. Three people have done it and all three are still
     here.
